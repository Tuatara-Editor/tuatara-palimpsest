#+title: Settings and limits
#+language: en

* Settings and limits

How much of a file palimpsest reads, and what it leaves unread.

palimpsest reads files from anywhere, some made to do harm. Everything it reads
is held to a size, so that no file can make it run without end or fill the
memory. What it leaves unread, the report says in =Not read= in the summary
(chapter 2). This chapter lists the settings that set the sizes, the limits that
are fixed, and what palimpsest does not do.

** The settings

Set them in the package's =:config= in your init file:

#+begin_src lisp
(load-package "palimpsest"
              :source "https://github.com/Tuatara-Editor/tuatara-palimpsest"
              :config
              (setf *palimpsest-most-decoded* (* 16 1024 1024 1024)))
#+end_src

~C-h v~ on a setting shows its help once the package is loaded. A size is a
whole number of octets, or words such as =512M=, =1.5G= or =20 GB=, in units of
1024.

#+table: settings *palimpsest-largest-file* *palimpsest-largest-stream* *palimpsest-most-decoded* *palimpsest-most-objects* *palimpsest-most-revisions* *palimpsest-largest-written* *palimpsest-largest-shown* *palimpsest-scan-pixels* *palimpsest-seconds*

A PDF larger than =*palimpsest-largest-file*= is not examined; it is read whole,
into memory, once. A stream that would decode to more than
=*palimpsest-largest-stream*= is read no further, and the same holds for a
page's content streams together. Past =*palimpsest-most-decoded*= nothing more
is decoded, a stream decoded again counting again. The revisions written out may
take =*palimpsest-largest-written*= together. Past it, or when the drive would
keep less than 1 GB free, a revision is listed as not written.
=*palimpsest-largest-shown*= bounds what ~C-c o~ shows of a stream.
=*palimpsest-scan-pixels*= bounds the scanned pictures whose words are measured,
and a larger one is passed over. A picture file whose header names more pixels
is refused by its header, before it is decoded.

The settings for the colours and lines of the drawing are in chapter 4.

** Each file read shut in

Everything palimpsest reads of a file it reads in a Tuatara of its own, shut in
by the sandbox: examining the file, showing one of its objects, drawing a copy
of it, and reading it to compare it with others. Each file has a Tuatara of its
own, which sees that file, read only, and one directory to write in. It has no
home directory, no network and none of your other files. The pictures it turns
into pixels are decoded there as well, each by a program held to its own files.
It sends back the report's words, its links and the files it wrote, and the
editor builds the report from those. Nothing it sends back is run.

Each of these Tuatara may take 1 GB of memory and three times
=*palimpsest-largest-file*= besides, 2.5 GB by default, and may run for
=*palimpsest-seconds*=, an hour by default. A file that needs more is stopped
there, and the editor carries on. Its report then says, under =Stopped=, for
example =The check of letter.pdf stopped: it needed more than 2,560 MB of
memory=, or that it took more than the seconds given. What was found before it stopped is
not kept. For an object shown or a copy drawn, the message says the same. Raise
=*palimpsest-largest-file*= or =*palimpsest-seconds*= to give a large file more
room, and close the report and examine the file again.

** Fixed limits

These are not settings:

- at most 5,000,000 cross-reference entries in all the sections of one file, and
  1,000,000 from one section;
- references followed 64 deep, the page tree read 64 deep, a node met twice
  passed over; a form's fields read 32 deep and 10,000 at most;
- forms drawn within forms followed 8 deep;
- at most 2,000,000 operators of one page, and 50,000,000 in all the pages of a
  file, every save's pages together;
- a font's ToUnicode map read for 131,072 codes at most, from a stream of 4 MB
  at most; the XMP metadata from a stream of 16 MB at most;
- a cross-reference section not where =startxref= says is looked for up to 64
  octets either side;
- at most 64 cross-reference sections followed by =/Prev=;
- at most 100,000 =%%EOF= lines outside streams read, 500 saves taken apart
  (=*palimpsest-most-revisions*=), and 5 saves inferred within streams compared
  with the save holding them;
- at most 12 known-genuine files set beside the examined file, their habits
  read from their first 3 pages; figures set against each other across the
  first 50 pages of a document.

Past a bound the report says so, under =Not read=.

** What palimpsest does not do

- *Encrypted files* are not decrypted, so their pages' content, fonts and
  pictures are not read. How the file is put together, its saves and its
  cross-reference sections are.
- *Signatures are not verified.* No certificate or digest is checked. The report
  gives what a signature claims and which octets it covers. The signer's name is
  read from the signature's own data, as data.
- *Pictures are decoded for the scanned pages alone*: a picture covering a third
  of a page or more. A JPEG or a JPEG 2000 is turned into pixels in the sandbox.
  A picture kept as fax or JBIG2 is drawn by poppler's =pdftoppm= in the
  sandbox, and one that cannot be read is named in the report. Other pictures
  are read only as their dictionaries say, and for a JPEG the metadata in its
  own segments.
- *Text in Type 3 fonts* with no ToUnicode map, and text drawn as pictures or
  shapes, is not read as text. Text drawn as shapes is not measured as ink
  either: only a scanned picture's pixels are. It shows as shapes the page
  draws.
- *Figures are read from text, never from pixels.* The checks of figures that
  must agree (check digits, totals, rows, net pay, balances, a figure stated
  twice, a weekday beside its date) read the text a file holds. A scanned page
  without a text layer has none, and its figures are not read from the picture:
  palimpsest runs no text recognition. Where a scan carries a text recognition
  layer, its figures are checked for the record only, as the layer may misread
  a digit.
- *Genuine files are set beside the examined file by their habits alone* (~C-c
  g~, chapter 5): how the files are put together and how their pages are
  laid out. Their words, figures and pixels are not compared, and no more than
  12 genuine files are read.
- *Positions follow the operators*: transformations, text matrices and spacing,
  and the widths each font gives. Clipping is followed as rectangles, the box
  around each clipping path.
- *What pages draw is checked in the latest revision.* The earlier revisions'
  pages are compared, each with the one before.
- *A field lock* is not read on its own. A certification's permissions are.
- *Text is read* through each font's ToUnicode map, else its encoding (the
  standard encodings and =/Differences=), else shown as the codes themselves in
  hex (=<0041>=).
- *Text is covered* where the filled shapes drawn after it hide each of its
  characters: the middle of each, along its own baseline however the text is
  turned, lying wholly where the shapes of one colour fill. Their outlines
  (curves cut into straight pieces) are filled row by row, half a point apart,
  by their fill rules within the outlines they are clipped to. Text is clipped
  by the box of what it is clipped to.
- *Content credentials (C2PA) are read, not verified.* Verifying them would mean
  checking the COSE signature over the claim, the certificate chain against the
  C2PA trust list, and the hashes that bind the claim to the file. Their JUMBF
  boxes and CBOR are read within limits of depth (8 boxes, 24 items), count
  (10,000 boxes, 200,000 items, each piece of a string counted) and size (64 MB
  of credentials, 4 MB of text kept, 32 octets of an object identifier). A CBOR
  map giving a key twice is refused. Past a limit the rest is not read, and the
  report says why. A WebP or HEIF picture is not examined, so its credentials
  are not read.
- *A JPEG's header* is set beside the device its EXIF names by signs only
  programs write (Adobe's segment, a progressive frame). JFIF before the EXIF is
  given for the record, as scanner drivers and phones write it too. No table of
  devices' own steps is kept: the one published survey (Kee, Johnson and Farid,
  2011) keeps its database private, and many devices write libjpeg's standard
  steps, so steps and Huffman tables are given for the record only, and a device
  that writes one of those signs itself is not known from one that does not. The
  EXIF is read within its segment, at most 200 entries a directory, each offset
  checked. Its thumbnail, at most 64 KB lying within the EXIF, beginning as a
  JPEG does and at most 1024 pixels across and down by its frame, is turned into
  pixels in the sandbox made no more than 320 across, and set beside the picture
  at that size, so a phone's 512 by 384 costs what a 320 by 240 does. A frame
  claiming more is refused for the record before it is decoded. A thumbnail
  shows an edit only when it is large in it: a region a sixteenth of the picture
  across is mostly missed. The two pictures are not drawn side by side; the
  octets to cut the thumbnail out by are given instead.

** Limits of the pixel checks

These bound what the checks of scanned pages and pictures can say.

- A scanned page's picture is turned into pixels to be examined: one kept
  without loss is read by the package, a JPEG or a JPEG 2000 by Tuatara's own
  decoder in its sandbox (ImageMagick or GraphicsMagick under bubblewrap), never
  in the editor itself. A picture kept as fax (CCITT) or JBIG2 is read by
  poppler's =pdftoppm= in the sandbox, from a PDF of that picture alone. One
  that cannot be read is named for the record, and nothing is claimed of it.
- A page kept as narrow strips drawn one under the next (of one kind and way up,
  abutting on the page) is joined into one picture first and named "the scanned
  page joined from N strips"; places are given in the strip that holds them.
  Strips that cannot be joined (two pictures at one strip's foot, too many
  pixels, more than 128 JPEG strips, JPEG blocks on no one grid) are read one by
  one, or named as not read. JPEG strips saved with other tables than the rest
  are worth a look.
- A scanned page is read line by line. A logo, a seal or a signature beside a
  few lines, whose ink joins their rows, is read apart from them where paper
  runs down between; a band no such gap parts is left unread, and the report
  says so. Text far lighter than the page's level of ink (thin type photographed
  in poor light) is not read at all, and a figure alone in a table's cell is
  read as characters, not judged as a word. Of 266 places pasted by hand into 19
  scanned documents, a word or a character is read at 249. A character's foot is
  judged only where its shape has four prints on the page and it has three
  neighbours on its line: 144 of those places.
- A black and white page (kept one bit a pixel, as fax, JBIG2 and image masks
  are, holding four grey levels or fewer) holds no grey. Its words are read and
  judged by how they are turned, but not by their ink, edges or paper, nor by
  where they sit, which one bit reads too roughly (honest fax pages at 200 dpi
  set words a pixel or two off their lines). Neither copies of a character nor
  characters without grain are told on it, as honest prints of a character come
  out the same pixel for pixel there (up to 89 pairs a page on the honest fax
  pages, 180 to 338 on JBIG2 pages). Only a run of three or more characters
  copied together is named. A word typed again in a matching face is not told on
  such a page.
- A photograph of a page, as a phone takes, lights, focuses and sharpens each
  print apart, so that prints of one character seldom come within 30 grey levels
  of each other; the page is then not judged, and the report says so. Its
  characters are still set beside one another against how alike its prints are
  within 60 grey levels, or the prints on their lines where those are more alike
  (other letters of a word copied with them not counted). A pair is named, worth
  a look, only when each is the other's nearest, alike to a quarter of that and
  three times as near as to the next of its shape, both at least half as tall as
  their page's type. They are also set beside the prints of judged pages, one
  way, and named when alike to a quarter of that page's likeness. A copy saved
  again with loss beyond that, one on a line too short to tell, a pair both
  clipped to one grey (bold type, a monospace column), or one cut apart
  otherwise than its source (a letter the blur joined to its neighbour), is
  missed there, and the page's words are not measured for grain.
- A picture an image generator made is told by its pixels only when it is kept
  without loss and at the size it was made: the pattern its decoder leaves every
  8 by 8 pixels is gone after a JPEG save, a print scanned again, or a picture
  made larger. Most generated pictures that reach a document are so changed, so
  a picture with no such finding is not thereby taken from life. Most office
  scans are not measured at all: their paper is made white to the last grey, and
  only paper short of white has the flat, unclipped pixels the pattern is read
  in. So a missing finding says nothing. A dither or half-tone screen on grey
  paper repeating every 8 or 4 pixels is far stronger than a decoder's pattern
  and is not named; one made faint enough to pass for it would be. Generated
  text that does not repeat as type does, blur and grain across a page, and a
  camera's own traces were measured and do not tell generated pages from honest
  ones (the numbers are in =doc/measures.org=).
- Text written with a chatbot is named only by the words it leaves behind when
  pasted and not read through. The style of the writing (words chatbots favour,
  sentences of even length) is not weighed: published detectors that weigh it
  name honest writers too often, above all those writing in a second language,
  and none has been measured on Swedish. Nothing in a PDF's structure names a
  chatbot either: text from one is saved by the word processor or browser it is
  pasted into. So a page without this finding may still hold such text.
- Check digits and totals are read from the page's text: the text a born-digital
  page draws, or the text recognition a scan carries. A scan without text
  recognition is not read for them. Plusgiro numbers are not checked, for want
  of a published account of their check digit; the length digit some OCR
  references carry is not checked either, as the bankgiro allows references
  without one. The lines of an invoice are not added up, nor the VAT set against
  its rate.
- Dates in the text are read in Swedish and English only, and only the
  document's own date by its label or a letter's date line. A date whose day and
  month could be swapped (05/03/2024), with a year of two figures, or written in
  other words (the fifteenth of March) is not read, nor a due date or a period.
  A date earlier than the file is never a sign, as a copy saved again later is
  honest; an invoice made out ahead of its date gives the sign honestly. A
  scan's text recognition may misread a figure, so its dates are for the record
  only. A date line written with figures alone (2017-11-20 on a line of its own)
  is not taken, as a due date set apart looks the same.
- A signature or a stamp copied is found only as a picture laid on whole: the
  same pixels, moved by whole pixels, neither scaled nor turned. One copied with
  a scale or a turn, printed and scanned again, laid over other ink (a line, a
  word) so that its mark joins that ink or is cut where words are taken out, or
  on a page kept as JBIG2 or drawn by a program, is missed; so is one smaller
  than twice the type, one copied into the same row or column of its page, and
  one on pages saved as JPEGs of quality 50 or worse. After a JPEG save the rule
  is looser, and two scans of one print (a facsimile signature a program prints,
  a stamp pressed by a machine) whose pixels happen to fall on its strokes
  within a tenth of a pixel, unturned, come as alike as a copy and are named.
- A page printed and scanned again defeats the signs read in its pixels. The
  printer and the scanner lay new ink, grain, edges and JPEG blocks over the
  whole page, the forged parts and the rest alike, so copied characters, retyped
  words, paper painted over and the traces of earlier saves are gone. What the
  new file says of itself, and what its words say, is all that is left to judge.

Chapter 7 ends with what the checks cannot prove.

#+begin_try Try it, changing a limit
1. Add a line to the =:config= of the =load-package= line in your init file that
   sets =*palimpsest-most-revisions*= to 2.
2. Restart Tuatara and examine a PDF with more than two saves.
3. Read =Not read= in the summary. It says that only the first saves were taken
   apart.
4. Take the line out again and restart.
#+end_try

** What palimpsest cannot see

Palimpsest reads what a file holds. A change that leaves nothing in the file
cannot be found, and a careful forger leaves little. These figures come from
87 forged documents made by another hand with other tools, at five levels of
care, each beside the honest document it was made from, and 18 honest copies
that went through the same tools unchanged (November 2026).

- Of the 87, palimpsest names the changed place in 36, says something worth a
  look elsewhere in 29, and says nothing in 22. At the two highest levels of
  care it names the place in 7 of 36.
- Of the 18 honest copies, 7 carry something worth a look, and 13 of the 52
  honest originals. None carries a strong sign. So worth a look means look, not
  forged.

What it finds well:

- A change kept beside what it replaced: a later save, a white box over the
  old figure, new text in a font the editor brought, a redaction with new text
  over it. All 4 files changed so with PyMuPDF, and 6 of 13 changed in the
  page's own stream, are named at the place.
- A figure changed without those that depend on it. A total that no longer adds
  up, a net pay that is not the gross less the deductions, a balance that no
  longer follows from the row before: these show after any printing or saving
  again. 14 of 22 files with one amount changed are named at the place.
- Pages put together from two exports, and a file changed where it lay after
  LibreOffice wrote it.

What it finds badly or not at all:

- A change made in the program that wrote the document, or by writing the page
  again with the font's own characters, every dependent figure worked out
  again: the file is then an honest file of other content. Draw 3 of 10, qpdf 1
  of 4.
- A document printed to PDF after the change (Chromium, CUPS, Ghostscript,
  cairo) or drawn again by Inkscape: the printing writes every page anew. Only
  figures that do not add up are left.
- A forgery flattened, printed and scanned again: 2 of 8. The scan's pixels
  hold the paper, not the edit; nothing local is left to find.
- A number, an address or a name changed with care: 3 of 18 and 1 of 5. No sum
  depends on them.
- Metadata written back to what the original said, when the file was written
  again afterwards.

Beside known-genuine documents of the same issuer (~C-c g~, chapter 5) the
same files say more. Of the 81 forged files that had such documents to stand
beside, the comparison alone names 60 as not the issuer's own, each worth a
look. With every check together, 5 of the 87 are then left with nothing said, in
place of 22. The price is that the comparison cannot tell a changed file from
one only written again: of 17 honest copies that went through the forgers' tools
unchanged it names 14. For that reason it never makes a strong finding. It says
the file is not as the issuer wrote it, which is true of them. Of 143 genuine
documents set beside others of their own issuer and template, it names none.

What stays unseen even so: a scan changed in its pixels and put back in the
scanner's own file, a page changed where it lay with the font's own
characters, a document made again in the issuer's own program. 21 of the 81
are such files.

An honest-looking report is therefore no proof that a document is honest. Set
the document beside its source where there is one: the issuer's own copy, the
bank's record, the register.
