* Examining examples: signed contracts

How a contract comes to be a PDF, and what palimpsest says of each way.

A contract reaches you as a PDF and the question is whether it is what the
parties signed. Each way of making and signing a contract leaves a file of its
own shape, and most of what palimpsest says only makes sense against that shape.
This chapter describes the two shapes of PDF, the common ways of making and
signing a contract, what palimpsest says of each, and what it cannot see. The
findings below are shown in a short form, as STRONG, LOOK and RECORD for the
three strengths of chapter 6, with the page.

** What a PDF holds, in two shapes

*A page made by a program* (a word processor, a drawing program, a PDF printer).
The PDF keeps the text as text: the page's drawing holds runs of characters with
their font and place, and the fonts are kept in the file. There is no object for
each paragraph or section. Text boxes and lines become runs inside the one
drawing of the page.

*A scanned page*. The PDF keeps a picture of the paper for each page, and a
drawing of one line that puts it over the whole page. Everything on the paper,
printed text, handwriting and stamps, is pixels in that picture. There are no
fonts, unless the scanner read the text (OCR) and laid an invisible text layer
over the picture. An office machine saving a compact PDF splits each page into
layers: a background picture, often a JPEG, and a black and white mask that
holds all the dark ink, kept as JBIG2 or CCITT. The signature is then in that
mask, with the printed text, and not apart from it.

So a pen signature is never an object of its own in an honest scan. A signature
that is a picture of its own, laid on a page, was put there by a computer.

Poppler's =pdfimages -list FILE= lists the pictures of each page, their size and
how they are stored, =pdffonts FILE= the fonts, and =pdfinfo FILE= the creator
and producer. palimpsest reads all of this itself. These are quick checks by
hand.

Three things follow from the table.

- Nothing survives paper. A contract written in a drawing program, printed,
  signed and scanned carries the scanner's name and not the program's. The
  drawing program as the creator of a scanned contract means someone opened the
  scan in it and exported it again, after it was scanned.
- One save proves little. Every program that writes a file anew writes one save.
  Exporting again throws away any history the scan had.
- A later save is where a viewer's signature goes. Signing in a viewer keeps the
  old file and adds the change after it (chapter 3).

** What palimpsest says of each

*** An honest scan

A scan of a signed contract from an office machine names the machine as the
producer and has no creator, one picture to a page and no fonts. palimpsest
says, for example:

#+begin_example
LOOK    p1  On page 1, a line of the scanned page has no grain of its own:
            drawn into the scan by a program, or in bold or heavier type, or
            ink, a stamp or coloured ink the scanner clipped.
RECORD      The ink's edges of the scanned pictures on pages 1 and 2 are as
            sharp as a scan's at about 300 dpi.
#+end_example

The worth-a-look is a title set in bold: heavy type holds its ink solid, with
less of the scanner's grain than the body text. The finding names that honest
cause itself. Open the page and look at the line it points to with ~d~: a title,
a bold heading or a dark stamp settles it, and an ordinary line of body text
would not. The record line says the scan is a real scan at the resolution the
file claims. This is what an honest scan looks like to palimpsest: nothing
strong, perhaps a look at heavy ink, and the resolution for the record.

*** A file written in one go

A contract written in a program and exported once, never changed, gets no
finding. It is what it says it is, and is the shape to compare the others with.

*** A scan saved again in a drawing program

Someone opened the scan to join, turn or change it and exported it. The creator
is the drawing program, and the pages are still one picture each with no fonts
and one save. palimpsest says what it said of the scan and may add:

#+begin_example
LOOK    p1  2 characters of the scanned page are nearer one another than
            prints of one character are: copied within the scan and saved
            again, or alike by chance, as clean prints on pages kept as JPEG
            or JPEG 2000, or smoothed, can.
#+end_example

The file's history is gone, so no save, layer or object tells what was done.
What is left to judge is the pictures. Two characters alike after a second JPEG
save happen. A word or a figure alike pixel for pixel would not. palimpsest does
not count a drawing program as the creator of a scanned page as a sign. You
should: ask who opened it and why.

*** A signature picture pasted into a page made by a program

A document made by a program may honestly carry a picture of a signature, and
with one picture there is nothing to set it against, for the pictures check
compares the pictures of a file with each other. palimpsest finds nothing. It
does not say "a signature on a page made by a program". You see it in the
structure: =pdfimages -list= shows one small picture on a page of text, at the
signature line. If the contract was said to be signed by hand on paper, this
file was not.

*** Signed later in a PDF viewer

The signature is a picture with its transparency in a stamp annotation, added in
a second save:

#+begin_example
STRONG  p2  A Stamp annotation (object 82) lies on page 2, added in revision
            2 (the current file): it can look like part of the page, but is
            laid over it.
LOOK        The file was changed after it was first saved: 1 later save is
            kept in it, beside what it changed.
#+end_example

The strong finding is a fact of the file and not a judgment: the signature is
laid over the page, in a later save. That is exactly what signing in a viewer
does, so it is honest when that is how the contract was meant to be signed. Open
revision 1 to see the contract as it was before, unsigned. When the signature
was said to be written on paper, it was not.

*** A page swapped

A page printed again with other terms, scanned in another run and put before the
signed page of the first scan, by the same machine, gets no finding beyond those
of the honest scan. This is a known miss. Two scans by one machine at one
setting look alike to every check, and pages swapped before the PDF was written
leave no save behind. What can show it is outside the file: the copy each party
kept, the page against a draft or an e-mailed version, the paper. Inside the
file, look at each page's angle and paper (the RECORD lines name the resolution
of each page) and read the terms against the signed page. Initials on every page
are why contracts ask for them.

** Signing services

A contract signed through a signing service or a bank's identity service carries
a cryptographic signature: a =/Sig= field whose octets cover the file as it was
signed. palimpsest reads what each signature claims, whether it covers its whole
save, and what any later save changed (chapter 7). Signing twice, or a service
adding its audit trail, adds later saves honestly. A change to the pages after
the last signature does not. palimpsest does not verify the certificate. Acrobat
or the service's own check does.

** A checklist

1. Before palimpsest, ask how the contract was said to be made and signed, and
   find its row in table 8.1.
2. Run =pdfinfo=. Do the creator and producer fit that way? A scan should name a
   scanner or a scanning app. A drawing program or word processor on a scan
   means it was opened and exported again.
3. Run =pdfimages -list=. One picture per page means a scan. A small picture on
   a page of text is a pasted signature.
4. Run palimpsest. Read the strong signs first: they are facts of the file, such
   as an annotation laid over a page or a page changed in a later save.
5. Read each finding worth a look with its honest causes, and open the place it
   names before deciding.
6. Open each earlier revision and compare it with the last.
7. Remember what palimpsest cannot see: pages swapped before the PDF was written
   and a pasted signature on a page made by a program. Compare with the other
   party's copy when it matters.

#+begin_try Try it, checking a contract
1. Open a signed PDF of your own, and run =pdfinfo= and =pdfimages -list= on it
   in a terminal. Decide which row of table 8.1 it fits.
2. Examine it with =M-x palimpsest= and compare the verdict with what you
   expected from that row.
3. If it has a later save, open revision 1 with ~w~ and see whether it is the
   contract before it was signed.
4. Press ~d~ and look at the place each finding names.
#+end_try
