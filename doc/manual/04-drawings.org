* Drawings

Seeing where each object is on the page, and the shapes of a scan's characters.

A finding that says "text on page 1 is covered" is easier to judge when you can
see the page. ~C-c d~ shows a copy of the PDF beside the report with each
thing the page draws framed and named by the object that draws it. For a scanned
page it also frames the places where signs were found in the pixels. ~C-c t~
shows the shapes of a scan's characters grouped, a view to look at that makes no
finding. This chapter covers both, how to keep what they show, and the colours
and lines of the frames.

** Objects framed: C-c d

~C-c d~ shows, in a window of its own down the right of the report, the PDF
with each thing its pages draw framed and named by the object that draws it. ~q~
there closes it. ~C-c d~ again, while it is shown, closes it too, and shows it
again at once later.

#+table: commands palimpsest-draw-objects

Text is no object of its own: a page's content stream draws it, a few operators
to a line. So each stretch of a line that one stream or form draws, without a
gap wider than one and a half times its size (a column apart), is framed on its
own and named by that stream. The name gives where in its decoded octets the
stretch's operators begin and the font objects it is set in. =4 @370 font 8=
means content stream 4, from octet 370 of it, in font object 8. ~C-c o~ and
then 4 shows the stream. A retyped figure often stands apart there: at the end
of the stream, or in a font of its own.

The findings about runs of text and lines, such as another size, bolder,
squeezed, spaced, another black, off the baseline or an edge, or a retyped
figure, name the same place after each detail, =(4 @370 font 8)=. ~RET~ on
=4 @370= shows object 4 with the cursor where those operators begin in its
decoded stream.

Other things are framed this way:

- each shape over 4 points a side, dashed;
- each picture, with the number of its content stream, form or picture above its
  top left;
- each annotation, dashed, with its number and kind (=15 FreeText=).

Every content stream, form and picture has a colour of its own, so a stream that
a later save appended stands out from the page's own. These colours are never
red, blue or green.

The copy shows the revision whose part of the report the cursor is in, else the
latest. It is that revision octet for octet, and one save more that holds the
drawing, the page's own drawing kept apart between =q= and =Q=. It is written to
the report's own directory, never beside the PDF examined, as
=objects-revision-N.pdf=. Where a font gives no widths, as the base fonts such
as Helvetica do not, the ends of its lines are reckoned and their frames may be
a little wide. An encrypted PDF is not drawn, since a stream added to it would
be read as encrypted too. The report stays where it is: read it while the page
is beside it, and use ~C-c o~ on a number to show that object.

A key at the foot of the page says what each colour means. Each line of the key
begins with a piece of its frame, so it holds whatever the settings below are
set to.

** Signs in a scan's pixels

On a scanned page ~C-c d~ also frames what the checks of chapter 7 name in the
pixels: words unlike the rest, copies, marks and words without grain of their
own, pieces saved as JPEGs, and characters not judged. Each is framed by its
strength:

#+caption: Table 4.1. How a frame is drawn as standard.
| Frame                 | Meaning                 |
|-----------------------+-------------------------|
| Red and whole         | A strong sign.          |
| Blue and dashed       | A sign worth a look.    |
| Dark green and dotted | A character not judged. |

The colour of each is a setting, =*palimpsest-strong-colour*=,
=*palimpsest-look-colour*= and =*palimpsest-not-judged-colour*=. A value is a
colour as ="#rrggbb"=, a name such as =:red=, or a number of the 256 colours.
How each is drawn is set by =*palimpsest-strong-line*=, =*palimpsest-look-line*=
and
=*palimpsest-not-judged-line*=, each =:whole=, =:dashed= or =:dotted=.

#+table: settings *palimpsest-strong-colour* *palimpsest-look-colour* *palimpsest-not-judged-colour*

#+table: settings *palimpsest-strong-line* *palimpsest-look-line* *palimpsest-not-judged-line*

A setting that holds no colour keeps the standard one.

A frame at the page's edge, on a scanned page of one picture, is drawn just
inside it, with its label within the page.

** A picture file

~C-c d~ works on a picture file (PNG, JPEG or TIFF) as it does on a scanned
page of a PDF. The picture is examined as one scanned page, and ~C-c d~ shows
that page with the signs found on it drawn over it, each framed by its strength
and labelled, the key at the foot of the page. Ink the scanner clipped is
hatched grey and characters not judged are framed in dark green. Only the signs
of the scan are drawn, because the page that stands for a picture holds the
picture alone, with no text, shapes or annotations to frame.

The drawing is written to the report's own directory, named after the picture:
=letter.png= gives =letter-drawing.pdf=, never beside the picture examined.
~C-c D~ writes it where you ask, offering =letter-drawing.pdf=. The picture is
read again from its file to draw it, and nothing is drawn when the file has
changed since it was examined.

** The shapes of the characters: C-c t

When a scan shows no copies but something still feels off, ~C-c t~ shows
beside the report the shapes of the characters of the PDF's scanned pages. It is
a view to look at, not a finding. palimpsest reads no text and claims nothing of
what it shows: honest type differs too, in bold, italic, headings, figures,
forms filled in and logos, and you look and decide.

#+table: commands palimpsest-show-shapes

Each scanned page's characters are cut out as for the copies of chapter 7 and
taken size by size. They are made black and white and scaled so that their size
is 32 rows. Two are grouped when, set together middle to middle or a pixel of
the scan either way, both their weight and their form agree. The pixels inked in
one of them only are under 0.3 of those inked in either, since a letter set bold
has more. Those more than a pixel of the scan from any ink of the other are
under 0.025 of them, since a letter of another typeface, or in italic, has
serifs, bowls or strokes elsewhere.

Each group is one row, with its count at the left and 32 characters at most, the
characters least like the first one first. Characters that match no other shape
of their size on their page come last, each with its line and its place in it:
=12:4= is line 12, the 4th character. Every character drawn leads to its place
on its scanned page. Those pages follow the pages of shapes in the same PDF,
with the characters that match nothing framed there with the same label.
Characters not judged, because other ink clips them, are framed dotted as
~C-c d~ frames them and labelled =not judged: clipped=, with the key line,
both where they are set and on the scanned page.

The view is the revision whose part of the report the cursor is in, else the
latest, with one save more, made in the background and written to the report's
own directory. The PDF itself is never changed. ~C-c t~ again closes the view.

** Keeping a drawing

~C-c D~ writes the copy with its objects framed to a PDF of its own, of the
revision the cursor is in, else the latest. It asks where, offering
=NAME-drawing.pdf= in the directory last saved to, else your home directory. It
never writes beside the PDF examined, writes nothing over a file, and makes the
file read only. The document information of the file says what it is, naming the
PDF, the revision and that each object it draws is framed, and that palimpsest
made it.

** Closing a drawing: C-c Q

~C-c Q~ in the report closes the copy with its objects drawn, or its pages of
shapes, when one is shown. Otherwise it gives the window back to what it showed
before, and the report is kept: =palimpsest= on the same PDF shows it again at
once, as ~C-x b~ does.

#+table: commands palimpsest-quit

The keys of the report are the letters after ~C-c~. Closing is ~C-c Q~ and
not ~C-c q~, which closes any read-only buffer.

#+begin_try Try it, drawing a page
1. Examine a PDF of your own with =M-x palimpsest=.
2. Press ~C-c d~. The page appears beside the report with frames and names.
   Press ~TAB~ to a finding that names a place like =4 @370=, and press ~RET~ to
   see the stream there.
3. Press ~C-c d~ again to close the drawing. Press it once more and it comes
   back at once.
4. If the PDF has a scanned page, press ~C-c t~ to see the shapes of its
   characters, then ~C-c t~ again to close them.
5. Press ~C-c D~ and give a directory of your own to keep the drawing.
#+end_try
