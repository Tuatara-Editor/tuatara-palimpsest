#+title: How the scan measures were chosen

The values of the scan checks were set by reading honest and
made-up scans. What was measured is kept here, by the definition it belongs to,
so that a value can be checked again or changed with its grounds in view.

The honest sets of the last round (November 2026) were 68 real documents: 19
public scans, 46 Swedish office scans from five makers' machines at 150 to 600
dots an inch, and 9 documents photographed with a phone or scanned at home;
292 scanned pictures, 248 of them with words to measure, 30,288 words. Beside
them were 62 PDFs made by programs (51 found on the system: manuals, test pages,
reference cards, written by Distiller, pdfTeX, LuaTeX, groff, Ghostscript, CUPS
and others; 11 written by LibreOffice and other programs), and honest made-up
scans. The forged sets were 46 made-up files (the practice samples, the
contract and the statement, the made-up scans with copies, pages with a word in
another face) and pages made by the tests' own generator for each rule.

* The scan checks

** page-level

On the honest sets (699 pictures) such rows were found on 113, nearly all
photographed with a phone. The level moved on 47 of them, by 1 to 3 grey levels
but on 7, by up to 24. The words read on the 113, their paper and ink read
without those rows too (PICTURE-WORDS), were 3,845 before and 3,820 after, and
1,022 and 1,025 on those scanned.

** *scan-measures*

Measured on the honest sets (649 pictures, 231 of real documents scanned, 381 of
real documents photographed with a phone, 37 made up), words named at these
values, and with the least or the multiple three quarters of it, came to the
following. Where it sits: 356, 409, 426. Turned: 0, 0, 0 (7 with the least
halved). Ink: 95, 135, 97. Edges: 332, 448, 498. Paper: 220, 306, 277. Honest
pages have such words (a heading, a line printed in another pass, a form filled
in), which is why each is worth a look at most; the values sit where a quarter
less names up to half as many honest words again. Made up and changed: a word
moved 6 pixels on a bowed page and a column 3 pixels lower are named still with
the least or the multiple half again as large; words pasted into the made-up
scans are named so by their ink, half of them by where they sit, none by their
edges or paper.

Measured again in November 2026 on the 248 honest pictures (30,288 words):
words named where they sit 352, by ink 77, edges 136, paper 192, turned none.
With the least three quarters as large, 418, 101, 185 and 243 (turned 3); with
the multiple three quarters as large, 412, 78, 189 and 206. With either half
again as large: 293 and 248, 23 and 23, 74 and 70, 105 and 92. The forged files'
words named by their edges (0.25 and 6) are all lost with either half again as
large, and those named by their ink half of them; a word pasted into a made-up
scan is named by its ink still with either half again as large, a word lowered
2 to 4 pixels by where it sits. So the edges and the paper stand where a quarter
less names a third more honest words and half again loses the forgeries.

A word lowered by 6 pixels, a third of its height, is not named, and from 7
pixels the line it sits in is named by its edges or paper instead of the word
by where it sits: the word is read as part of a line of its own height, not set
off the line it left. A limit of how lines are read, not of these values.

** unlike-words

Honest pages cut to their first words, in reading order, named this share of
their words: 6 words 1.3 %, 8 words 5.0 %, 10 words 2.1 %, 12 words 3.9 %, 16
words 4.1 %, 24 words 4.5 %, where whole pages name 2.5 %. The first lines of a
page are its heading and letterhead, set apart honestly; no count under 12 names
fewer, and none of the forged files' words named lies in a page of fewer than
40 words. The 12 words keep the usual from being taken from a handful.

** Line structure

*scan-column-mates* (6), *scan-widest-word* (5), *scan-column-rows* (5),
*scan-region-lines* (6) and *scan-bow-lines* (6), each set lower and higher,
named this many words of the 248 honest pictures, against 757 at the values
used: column mates 4 and 9, 750 and 756; widest word 4 and 7, 709 and 787;
column rows 4 and 7, 767 and 754; region lines 4 and 9, 751 and 796; bow lines
4 and 9, 757 and 757. The forged files' and the made-up forgeries' words were
named the same at every one of them (two more forged words at a widest word of
7). Bow lines at 9 no longer name the form whose values sit lower than their
labels on every line of a bowed page (8 words, named in pairs as one moved or
a form filled in); at 4 nothing changes. No value of these parts the honest
words named from the forged ones; each is set by what it is for, and moving it
moves the honest count by 6 % at most.
** *band-least*

Measured on the honest sets: 699 pictures, 231 of real documents scanned, 381
photographed with a phone, the rest made up at grain 1 to 3, bold, clipped,
blurred, kept as JPEG, lit unevenly. There was no band on a real document. Three
made-up forms had one, their shaded header row, about 20 levels darker than the
paper above and below it, as honest pages have. That is why a band is worth a
look at most. Measured on bands of lines printed again over 13 kinds of made-up
page: serif, sans, bold and DejaVu type, one to four lines, 14 to 28 lines a
page, light falling across, JPEG of quality 75 and 90, 200 dots an inch, kept
without loss or as JPEG. Every band from half its grey to 0.96 of it was named
once. A word pasted into it was named on its own besides. The floor depends on
how much of the page the band is, as the rows about it are its measure. On pages
of 21 lines, bands of two lines were named to 0.97 (seeds 41 to 46). On pages of
12 lines, bands of two or three lines were named to 0.96, bands of four lines to
0.92, and missed from 0.94 (three seeds). At 0.97 a band is missed on some pages
at 200 dots an inch kept as JPEG of quality 90.

** *scan-region-paper*

On the honest sets (649 pictures) blocks of ink named at 1 grey level, 2 more
than at 2; at 3 or 4, as many as at 2, and every band of the made-up series
named still.

** scan-picture-read

The page's tilt read so was the first reading's on all 179 made-up pages with a
band tried. A band read as it is spoils the reading of the whole page. At 200
dots an inch and half its grey, its paper (123) lies below the midpoint of the
page's darkest ink and its level, so its rows are not left out of the level
(PAGE-LEVEL). It pulls the level from 195, read with it lifted, to 181, and the
page's darkest fiftieth from 114 to 71, against which every word's baseline is
measured. Words far from the band were set off their lines by 1.6 to 1.8 pixels.

** rule-piece-p

Measured on the faces of the made-up scans (Liberation Serif and Sans, regular
and bold, DejaVu Sans): the tallest characters, j, brackets and the bar |, reach
1.35 to 1.4 times the height of the capitals, under half again. The narrowest
stand 2.5 to 4.6 times as tall as wide before the scanner's blur widens them, l
at 2.7 to 3.3, the bar alone past 4. An l stands no taller than the letters that
rise above the others, which UPPER-HEIGHT measures however few they are. So the
bar, and j and brackets in a narrow sans face (3.3 to 3.7 times as tall as
wide), taller by a tenth and narrow, are taken as pieces of rules too, and are
not set beside others for copies: a limit. A piece of a rule 4.2 times as tall
as wide and 1.42 times the type, as a photographed form gave, is told; one cut
to the height of the type, or 2.65 times as tall as wide, is not, and is set
beside the characters as one.

** grain-levels

on the practice scans honest characters stray by 5 or more where the typical is
17 to 19

** bare-glyphs

Of 720 honest pages of made-up scans none had fewer than 216 characters
measured. 676 had 2 % or fewer of them without grain, and 28 from 2 to 5 %. 16
had more than a twentieth (small type against a table's rules, bold type the
scanner clipped, prints with no blur), on which words without grain cannot be
told from the page's own. Pages with words typed onto them hold 0.5 to 1.5 %
without grain.

On the real honest pages (170 pages of characters measured) the fewest
characters measured on a page was 152. 125 pages had 2 % or fewer without
grain, 18 from 2 to 5 %, and 27 more than a twentieth, on which nothing is told;
none of them named a word or line without grain. The forged files' pages held
3.1 % at most.

** same-strips

Of 720 honest pages the most strips of one number on a page with grain was 11,
and 291 to 333 on pages drawn by a program set in a file beside a scan. On the
real honest pages judged by their likeness no number came twice; the most on
the forged files was 3, on the made-up stamps 4.

** join-exact-copies: no size makes a pair strong

Pairs the same pixel for pixel on a JPEG's grid, on a stepped page or on a page
of faint grain stay worth a look however much ink they share. Measured in
November 2026, the size of a pair being the pixels of ink of the widest block
the two share:

- The real honest sets (76 files) gave no pair the same pixel for pixel.
- 228 honest pages, made up or taken from real scans. The made-up ones hold
  text in three faces, one face in nine sizes from 7 to 20 pixels regular and
  bold, small type and tables of figures, at grain 1 to 7; 8 pages of real scans
  were taken too. Each was made faint or stepped by a JPEG of quality 30 to 90
  decoded and kept without loss, posterising to 4 to 16 levels, 1-bit made grey
  and saved as a JPEG of quality 50 to 95, JPEG 2000, or kept as a JPEG of
  quality 50 to 95. They gave 925 pairs on 19 pages, of 20 to 48 pixels of ink,
  21 in the middle and 26 at nine tenths, in blocks up to 8 by 10 pixels; on the
  grid of pages kept as a JPEG, 22. Most came from posterising to 12 or 16 levels.
- 84 forged pages of the same kinds, 40 characters each copied with a margin of
  2 to 6 pixels, laid with Darken or pasted, half of them a whole number of
  8-pixel blocks from their source, then saved as a JPEG of quality 50 to 95,
  kept as it is or decoded and kept without loss: 219 pairs, 20 to 97 pixels of
  ink, 26 in the middle, 36 at nine tenths; 9 over 48, 3 of 64 or more.

The honest pairs reach 48, past nine tenths of the copies, so no size keeps them
out and the copies in. The figure of the forged statement found on the grid
shares 64; a limit there would make 3 of 219 copies strong, a third above the
largest honest pair on type 7 to 20 pixels high, where type twice that height
gives honest blocks larger still.

One honest page gave a strong sign: a table of figures at grain 1, saved as a
JPEG of quality 30 and kept without loss, not told stepped, had 19 characters
named copies, figures a whole number of 8-pixel blocks apart that came out the
same (pairs of 22 to 41 pixels of ink). Marking pairs on the grid by their places
alone, on any picture, took that away, but also the strong sign of a mark at one
place on every page, of letters on a fixed pitch of 8 pixels, of a date stamp's
figures and of copies laid 8 pixels apart; it is not made. The page is told
once a JPEG by its frequencies instead (JPEG-HISTORY, below), with the corner
its grid begins at, and its pairs a whole number of blocks apart on that grid
are worth a look, as on a page kept as a JPEG; pairs at any other offset stay
strong, as a piece pasted at a chance place is 63 times in 64. A page whose
characters show the JPEG's blocks (STEPPED-PICTURE-P) is stepped then only on
that grid: an independent reviewer pasted four characters 3 across and 5 down
on pages of grain 3 and 7 once saved at quality 50 and 90, and they stayed
worth a look until this was done. Ink alike to the pixel without its paper
stays worth a look at any offset there, as a JPEG of quality 30 flattens the
cores of clean prints wherever its grid falls: counted by the grid, 3 honest
made-up pages gave strong signs that way. Measured again
with that: no strong sign on the real honest sets but the two Stamp
annotations laid on by a later save; none on the 228 honest pages of the
break set; none on 188 made-up pages saved as JPEGs of quality 30 to 98 and
kept without loss, of which 16 at grain 0 to 0.5 gave strong signs before, 2
to 98 characters each. Of the forged pages, two kept without loss after a JPEG
of quality 90 lost their strong sign (2 and 6 characters): every pair found
on them lay whole blocks apart, as the generator lays half its copies.

Limits found on the way, on main as on this branch, not made here:

- Pages drawn by a program without grain over a grid every 8 or 16 pixels
  (lines dark, grey or light, text in Liberation Sans, blur 0.6): 7 of 45
  made-up pages give a strong sign on prints drawn the same, never saved as
  JPEGs; main gave 26 on those pages and their JPEGs, this branch 8.
- Made-up scans of grain 3 made 1-bit without dithering, then blurred by a
  Gaussian of 0.7: 2 of 8 (sizes, 43 characters; a table, 12) give a strong
  sign. An independent generator at grain 2 to 3 did not reproduce it.

** picture-likeness

On 939 pages of made-up scans, reading the marks standing alone moved a page's
likeness by 6 % at most, by under 1 % on 19 pages of 20. There, 80 characters
taken at even steps moved it by up to a quarter. Measuring 400 rather than 80
takes a tenth longer on a scan of 60 pages.

** Pages drawn on a grid: RULED-GRID-P and *ruled-grain-most*

Born-digital pages drawn over a grid every 8 or 16 pixels, without grain,
had their copies strong (26 groups on main, 8 after once-JPEG pages were
told): the grid crosses each print where it falls, so their prints differ
(alike to 10 to 17 grey levels, where the same page without the grid gives
0) and two are the same where they fall alike on it. Their blank paper reads
as straying by 8 to 13 for the grid lines, so they were not drawn by a
program. The paper is now also measured pixel by pixel, each against the
mean of its two neighbours across, down or on either slant, the least of the
four: a line drawn runs the same along its length. Pages drawn on grids give
0 to 0.38, with grain of 0.5 added 0.15 to 0.85; scans of grain 2 and more
give more, and many real scans whose paper was whitened 0. A grid in the
blank paper (RULED-GRID-P) is told by the mean greys of its columns and its
rows, gathered by place within every 8 or 16 pixels, lying 4 grey levels apart
or more: every page drawn on a grid of 8 or 16 and none of the 264 real
scanned pictures. A page with both is drawn by a program, its characters not
judged. One page saved as a JPEG of quality 30 strays by 3.5 for the JPEG's
ringing and stays strong in one pair, as on main: a limit.

** *likeness-same-most* and FLAT-COPIES: prints that share their ink

Pages made black and white without dithering from scans of grain 3, then
smoothed by 0.7 pixels, gave 12 and 43 characters strong: two prints are the
same wherever their black and white fell alike, and differ elsewhere by 14 to
22 grey levels, judged as prints. The nearest prints of a character on such
pages share 0.82 to 0.94 of their ink grey level for grey level; on 185 real
scanned pictures 0.02 to 0.09, on made-up scans of grain 1 to 7 up to 0.31,
on posterised pages 0.43 to 0.98. A group of copies is worth a look
(FLAT-COPIES) when, for half its characters or more, the prints on the
character's own line, in no group of copies nor stamp, share half their ink
or more with their nearest; where a line holds fewer than three such, the
prints in groups but for those the same in all their ink, else the five
nearest its row in no group. Judged over eight type heights, a group on the
last line of a black and white band took in the grey lines below it and
stayed strong (19 characters on one honest page). Judged by line, the
honest pages made black and white in their top fifth to two thirds give no
group strong, and copies with grain laid 1 to 10 type heights below such a
band, or in its grey part, stay strong (22 to 24 characters): a line of
copies laid whole holds no prints but copies, and the copies the same in all
their ink are left out. A stamp's figures drawn alike are left out too.

** stepped-picture-p

On honest made-up pages so made, of grain 2 to 3 grey levels and judged by their
likeness, a JPEG of quality 30 kept without loss made a pair of prints the same
pixel for pixel on 5 of 6, 16 levels on 5 of 6.

** jpeg-history: a picture kept without loss that was once a JPEG

A JPEG leaves every frequency of its 8 by 8 blocks near a whole number, on the
grid it had, however fine its quality; JPEG-HISTORY measures the mean cosine
of a whole turn times each frequency 2 or more from nothing, at the best of
the 64 places the grid can begin, and how many standard deviations that sum
stands above frequencies spread evenly. Measured in November 2026, a picture
counted as told when 5 standard deviations or more:

- Never saved as JPEGs were 96 made-up scans (text, tables of figures, one face in
  nine sizes, bold and small type at grain 0 to 3, grain 0 born digital with
  and without blur, posterised to 4 to 16 levels, 1-bit with and without
  dithering, 1-bit blurred), the break set's 94 pages made faint or stepped
  without a JPEG (posterised, JPEG 2000, 1-bit) and the 11 pictures of the
  real honest sets kept without loss, giving at most 0.040 where told,
  but for one colour page of the real scans at 0.09 on the grid from its
  corner, 7 of its lowest frequencies on steps of 4 to 11 as a JPEG's are: a
  page most likely once a JPEG, its sum just under 5 standard deviations.
  None was named.
- Drawn by programs on a period of 8 pixels and never saved as JPEGs, 45
  pages of text over a grid every 8 or 16 pixels (dark, grey or light lines),
  a form ruled every 24 and a table whose cells are multiples of 8, grain 0 or
  0.5, blur 0 or 0.6; and an independent reviewer's 25 pictures never saved
  (grids, halftones, moiré, pictures enlarged by repeating pixels). None was
  told, the most 0.020. Before blocks the same pixel for pixel were counted once, such a page
  with a grid of grey lines, blurred and without grain, gave 0.106 at 48
  standard deviations: its blocks repeat exactly, and their few frequencies
  fall near whole numbers by chance a thousand times over.
- Saved as JPEGs of quality 30, 40, 50, 60, 75, 85, 90, 95 and 98 by libjpeg,
  decoded and kept as PNG, a third cropped by 1 to 7 pixels after: 188 of 188
  named, 0.153 to 0.204, 0.187 in the middle; the grid found where it now
  begins on 187. The pages drawn on a period of 8 so saved: 89 of 90, 0.164 to
  0.204. Of the break set's pages kept so, tables at grain 1 among them, 54
  of 56 were named, and 7 of 8 real scans kept so.
- Those missed hold too few blocks free of clipped pixels to tell (2.6
  standard deviations or fewer): pages whose paper a scanner had made white,
  where a JPEG of low quality rang about every letter past white.

So 0.08 is the cut (*history-lattice-least*): twice the largest honest value,
half the least once-saved one. Frequencies from 1 rather than 2 gave
honest clean pages of grain 0.5 up to 0.09, the pixels a level from the paper
making sums of a cosine or two (*history-least*). The steps of the table are
found too (HISTORY-STEP), for the record: 5 to 18 of the lowest 18 frequencies
on pictures saved at quality 95 or less, but up to 18 too on pages drawn on a
period of 8 and never saved, so they decide nothing; at 98 the steps are
mostly 1 and only the whole numbers tell. A page of strokes set
exactly upright, every row of a block the same, multiplies the decoder's
rounding by the root of 8 and is missed at quality 30: told less often, never
more. It costs 0.1 seconds for a picture in the middle, 0.5 at most, once for
each picture kept without loss.

** Local traces: LOCAL-TRACES, *lattice-region-least*, *requantised-region-least*

Measured in November 2026 on the forged-scan set (72 made-up forgeries, 25
honest references; 41 forged copies of real scans with 20 unchanged
controls), the real honest sets (19 public scans, 44 Swedish office scans, 9
phone and home scans), the break set (228 honest pages made faint or stepped)
and 22 honest pages saved as JPEGs twice with no change between, a third of
them cut by 1 to 7 pixels between the saves. The page is cut into tiles of 16
by 16 pixels on its grid of blocks; a tile 2 deviations or more short of the
page joins its neighbours that are, and the region is judged on all its tiles
together.

- A page kept without loss but once a JPEG (JPEG-HISTORY): the mean cosine of
  a whole turn times the frequencies of each tile's blocks, set beside the
  page's. The break set's 54 pages so kept gave at most 5.1 deviations; the
  made-up forgeries saved as PNG gave 18.5 to 54.6 at the forged place, 23.5
  in the middle (6 of 6: two retyped, two cloned, two stamps or signatures
  laid on). So 10 is the cut, twice the largest honest value. Growing the
  regions over tiles 1 deviation short instead of 2 raised honest pages to
  10.3, so regions grow only over tiles 2 short.
- A JPEG saved twice, coarser first: the steps of its lowest 15 frequencies
  left all but empty over the whole picture (EMPTY-STEPS). Saved once, the
  made-up references gave at most 9 such steps, the real scans 5, the break
  set 3, the real-based controls 10 but one at 66 (a device that saves twice);
  saved twice with coarser steps first, 34 to 346. So 30 is the least
  (*requantised-steps-least*). A JPEG turned a quarter as its EXIF says has
  its frequencies across and down swapped; read with its table as kept, an
  honest one saved once at quality 75 gave 36 empty steps, though no region
  stood out (4.2). The table is read turned when the frequencies lie nearer
  its steps so. The share of each tile's values on those steps
  is set beside the page's by the signed root of the Poisson deviance: a share
  set beside it by its normal deviation let one tile of four values on empty
  steps, where the page held 0.3%, reach 40 deviations on honest pages. Honest
  pages saved twice, cut or not, gave at most 7.0, the honest regions of the
  forged ones 7.2 and the real-based control saved twice 4.0 (an
  independent review's own honest pages gave at most 4.8 here and 4.9 on
  lossless pages); regions laid in
  between the saves 9.2 to 84.6, 21 in the middle (8 of the 9 made-up
  forgeries on such pages, one real-based). So 9 is the cut.
- A part of an honest page filtered after the save gives the same sign: a
  JPEG of quality 85 kept as PNG with a median of 3 by 3 over a band of 200
  pixels along its top gave 23.8, as a scanner removing specks along an edge
  would. The finding names that cause.
- Saved a second time at the same quality, or coarser, a JPEG leaves no empty
  steps, and those forgeries (11 made-up, saved by the scanner's own writer)
  are not told this way. Pages never saved as JPEGs have no traces to lack.
- No honest file of any set was named: no new finding on the 345 honest
  files and the 20 controls. Made-up forgeries found at the forged place
  rose from 16 to 28 of 72, real-based from 11 to 12 of 41.
- It costs 0.2 to 0.3 seconds a page at 300 dots an inch, up to 1.2 for a
  JPEG of high quality whose grid is searched for (*requantised-corner-off*).

Also measured, and not kept as findings:

- The paper's noise and its spectrum, region by region: the root mean square
  of the paper's pixels (ink and 3 pixels round it left out) less the mean of
  their four neighbours, and its ratio to the same less the mean of 5 by 5,
  each tile set beside the tiles about it (or along its row). Honest
  references reached 23 to 41 deviations, at bands of table rows, shaded
  headings and the paper between lines of bold type; the forged regions 4 to
  21 but for a splice from another scan (41). Nothing told them apart.
- The paper round two characters of the same shape, set side by side: the
  correlation of their paper's pixels less the mean of 3 by 3 or 5 by 5, ink
  and 1 to 3 pixels round it left out, at the best of 9 shifts of a pixel, for
  each character with every other of its size and ink on the page. Honest
  pages reached 0.87 (0.59 with 100 pixels of paper or more): the blur round
  the strokes of characters alike in shape is alike. Characters copied and
  saved as JPEGs gave 0.24 to 0.56 at the forged place, those kept without
  loss 0.82 to 0.99, which the copy search already names. A JPEG save takes
  the paper's grain with it as it does the ink's.
- Paper the same pixel for pixel as paper elsewhere, as a clone stamp or a
  content-aware fill leaves on a page kept without loss: blocks of 8 by 8
  pixels of paper every 4 pixels, found again anywhere on the page by a
  rolling hash, three or more side by side the same distance off. Smooth
  paper repeats honestly: pages once JPEGs of low quality or JPEG 2000 and
  kept without loss, posterised pages and a public scan gave hundreds of such
  regions. Kept to blocks of 10 grey levels or more whose pixels stray 1.5 or
  more from their neighbours, honest pages still gave one region of 5 blocks
  (a JPEG 2000 page of the break set, grain 3.3) beside forged ones of 3 to
  22 blocks, a fill of 4. Too near to be named.

** copied-glyphs

On the made-up scans so set the paper strays by 0 to 0.4 and the cores by 13 to
30.

Scanned paper not made white strays by nearly 1 or more (0.97 on a denoised
page). The tallest characters of type (a bar, brackets, a j) are 1.32 to 1.36
times the taller type in the faces tried (DejaVu, Nimbus Sans Narrow,
Liberation Serif); the stamps tried stand at 1.97, signatures and bar codes at
2.0 to 2.1, so a group taller by half than the type is taken as laid on.

On the real honest pictures the least of the typical spread of the cores and
the paper's spread under half a grey level met once: one office scan of the
Swedish set, cores straying 1 and paper 0.35, is taken as drawn by a program.
That only keeps its findings worth a look at most. The nearest others: cores 2
with paper 0.06 (a phone scan), cores 1 with paper 0.86. 50 of 247 real
pictures had paper straying under half a grey level, a scanner's white point,
their cores straying 2 or more. Of the 101 groups of copies the real honest
pages gave, all worth a look, none was taller than 1.4 times the type, so none
was named laid on; the stamps and signatures tried stand at 1.97 and more.

** check-scans

On honest made-up pages made with a JPEG kept without loss or few grey levels,
of grain 0.7 to 4 grey levels, 32 of 169 had a pair of prints the same pixel for
pixel. Honest pages of small print are read at about three fifths of the
characters their ink is reckoned to hold, so a page is named as partly read
only below half.

** split-joined

In the faces tried (DejaVu Serif, Sans, Condensed and Mono, Nimbus Sans Narrow,
Liberation Serif, bold too) a W, an M, an m, an @ or a % is 1.5 to 2.2 times the
typical width, and two narrow letters run together (il, ii, ij) 0.8 to 1.4
times it.

On the real honest pages the marks fall evenly across the value: of 170,000
marks between one and two and a half times the typical width, 5,230 stand at
1.4 to 1.5 and 4,674 at 1.5 to 1.6. No gap parts letters from pairs on real
pages; half again is where the faces tried part them, and a piece told a copy by
its likeness alone is worth a look at most, so a wrong cut costs no strength.

** *glyph-least-shape*

Pairs of characters on the real honest pages alike enough by their ink to be
copies (782 pairs not the same pixel for pixel): 560 have crossings under 0.2,
strokes alone, and are told by the paper about them only. With the least at a
third, 198 pairs are named worth a look; at a quarter 212, at a fifth 222, at
half 187. On the forged files' 149 such pairs (copies saved again among them):
97 at a third, 102 at a quarter, 75 at half. A third keeps the forgeries a half
would lose and names 14 fewer honest pairs than a quarter.

** JPEG blocks: *block-grid-least*, *block-grid-above*, *block-smooth-least*

A whole made-up page saved as a JPEG and kept without loss since, at quality
50 to 95 and grain 1.5 to 7: its characters' blocks on the grid typically have
22 to 28 of the 28 finest frequencies flat, 8 to 28 more than at the other
placements, and the grid is found on 15 of 16; at quality 95 and grain 7, 14
flat, it is not. The real honest pictures kept without loss (10) typically have
0 flat (one 6), none with a grid. 58 of the real pictures kept as JPEGs carry
their grid, as they should. Smooth throughout, at 14 or more flat, a picture is
not searched for pieces: of the made-up and real scans kept without loss only
one, made up, was.

** DRAWN-STAMP-P and *stamp-grain-most*

A stamp drawn by software whose figures include a plain one, a bar as a 1 is,
was strong: plain strokes are never judged copies, so the 1s were in no group
and the stamp failed the rule that every repeated shape lies in one group.
Prints of a shape the same pixel for pixel now count as drawn too; a rubber
stamp pressed and scanned never prints a figure twice the same pixel for pixel,
and one with a figure copied over another stays strong.

Shapes are told by the ink of the prints, not by their size alone: figures of
one size but of different shapes lie 76 to 187 grey levels apart (the root of
the mean squared difference of their ink), prints of one drawn figure 0, and
prints of one figure of a rubber stamp pressed and scanned 16 to 18, under the
20 a shape takes in. So a figure printed once, a 6 among 0s, stands alone and
does not count against the stamp. A stamp needs one shape repeated, not two:
a date stamp of 2026 repeats only its 2.

page's characters of the group's size outside any stamp: judged over the

and a recolouring that keeps one colour's grain drops some of it in grey, so

** *edge-scanned-widest* and the resolution of a scan

The width of an edge of ink, between a fifth and four fifths of the way from
paper to ink, was measured on the honest pages with 1,000 edges or more. On
209 scanned on flatbeds and office machines at 150 to 600 dots an inch, the
middle width of a page was 0.8 to 1.9 pixels on 206 of them. Three were
wider, 2.3 to 4.4, of two documents made larger or blurred before they were
put out, which of the two is not known. On 389 photographed with phones it
was 0.7 to 2.9, 2.0 in the middle; the 164 wider than 2.0 are named as
holding the detail of a lower resolution, as a phone's camera blurs.

Eighteen honest pages were cut to 800 by 800 pixels and made larger,
smaller and larger again with ImageMagick. Their edges grew with the scale:

| Made                                       | Width over the page's own |
|--------------------------------------------+---------------------------|
| half as large again, bicubic               | 1.38 to 1.68              |
| twice as large, bicubic, Lanczos or linear | 1.64 to 2.48              |
| twice as large, then JPEG 75 or 92         | 1.77 to 2.12              |
| four times as large, bicubic               | 3.43 to 3.76              |
| half as large, then twice again            | 1.17 to 2.36              |
| a quarter as large, then four times again  | 1.93 to 4.02              |
| half as large                              | 0.69 to 1.14              |
| kept as JPEG 50 or 75                      | 0.95 to 1.15              |
| a 3 by 3 median                            | 1.00 to 1.88              |
| blurred by a Gaussian of 1.5 pixels        | 1.69 to 3.83              |

A page blurred grows its edges as a page made larger does, and loses as
much detail when it is made half as large and back: 0.023 to 0.060 of the
span from paper to ink blurred, 0.025 to 0.052 made a quarter as large and
four times again, 0.056 to 0.221 as it was. The grain of the blank paper
set beside its neighbour's was missing on most pages, their paper made
white, and where it was there it overlapped: 0.07 to 0.51 as they were,
0.42 to 0.71 made twice as large, 0.44 to 0.89 made four times as large.
Honest pages at 400 and 600 dots an inch blurred by a Gaussian of 1.2
pixels, as a scanner whose optics are softer than its pixels blurs, came to
2.1 to 2.3. So a page whose edges are wider than *edge-scanned-widest* (2.0)
is said to hold the detail of a lower resolution: made larger, blurred, or
scanned by such a scanner, for which the span of resolutions given does not
hold.

The resolution it holds the detail of is given as from its pixels' times
0.6 over its edges' width (the sharpest edge a picture can have, a step
within a pixel, which a page made smaller keeps) to its pixels' times 1.9
over it (the widest honest edge). A page whose edges are as narrow as a
scan's is said to have edges as sharp as a scan's at the resolution of its
pixels: scanned at that, made smaller from a finer scan, or made larger from no less than its pixels'
times 0.6 over its edges' width. It is never said flatly to be scanned at
that resolution: pages made smaller keep their edges as sharp, and sharp
pages made 1.33 to 2 times larger with bicubic, Lanczos or Mitchell blending
came to edges of 1.2 to 2.0. Of 257 made pages whose resolution before is
known, what was said held for 256. The one it missed was made
twice as large by repeating its pixels, whose edges stay sharp; such a page
has every change from one pixel to the next on one place in every two, and
is named by that (REPEATED-PIXELS).

The pixels to the inch a JPEG says disagreed with the page's on 4 honest
pages, all saying 96, what programs write when given none. The letters of
the honest pages, their capitals' height at the page's pixels to the inch,
came to 6.2 to 9.6 points on pages of text and up to 48 on pages of
headings, too spread to tell a wrong page size by.

Working it out adds about 0.06 s a million pixels to reading a scanned
page: 0.18 s for an A4 page at 150 dots an inch, 0.53 s at 300, 1.8 s at
600.

** judge-tail

Of the same 15,475 honest PDFs, 14,686 ended their last %%EOF with a line
feed, 447 with a carriage return and line feed, 101 with a carriage return
and 230 with nothing; none of the 20 made for the round, and none of the
others, had a single octet of white space after that line end: no newlines
more, no spaces, no padding with NULs. A mail program or a transfer in text
mode may add one more line end, so four octets or fewer of line ends and
spaces are nothing. More is worth a look, as white space that can hold data:
a few hundred NULs or spaces carry a file's worth of bits. A kilobyte or more
is strong, as Adobe's readers look for %%EOF only in a file's last 1,024
octets, so no program whose files open there writes it; the 1.4 MB a reviewer
hid there is far past it. So are 64 octets or more mixing two or more kinds
of white space (spaces, tabs, NULs, form feeds, carriage returns, line feeds),
the way a code hiding bits in white space writes them (eight octets' worth of
bits at one a character; 900 octets of carriage returns and line feeds at
random hold about 110): no honest file had even one of them there.

One octet repeated, after one line end at most, holds no data at any length,
so it is worth a look, named as padding: a file copied to a block's size or
carved out of a disk ends with NULs to the end of its cluster (3,431 in a
4,096-octet cluster), and old transfers padded with Ctrl-Z (0x1A) to a
128-octet block. Those were strong at 1 KB, or at 16 octets for Ctrl-Z, which
is not white space; with a single octet repeated there is nothing to read.

(the hour of a twelve-hour clock: made at 07:52, changed at 19:57 the day
before). At a minute those were strong signs. Such a gap is now worth a look,
a clock or a time zone named as a cause, only when it fits one (CLOCK-SLIP-P):
the two dates in different zones or one without a zone, up to 14 hours apart;
an hour or less; or within a quarter of an hour of 12 hours. Every other gap
stays strong: 6 hours with both dates at +01:00, 13 hours in one zone, 17
hours across two zones, and anything over a day, as the made-up file changed
25 hours before made. The newest date of an honest file was 13 days before it
was read.

* The cases

** *cases-alike*

Of the 62 PDFs made by programs, 121 pairs share a way of editing and either a
second way or a tool; 66 of them are alike to a half or more and so one case,
up to 0.71 for unrelated files (pdfTeX beside groff), sharing two signs worth a
look that honest files give (text placed off its line, runs fitted), not a
forger. Pairs of the forged files that qualify are alike 0.1 to 0.4, four of
them 0.7 and more. No value of likeness parts the honest pairs from the forged
ones; a case is grouped by signs worth a look as well as strong ones, so files
of one directory that honest programs made can be grouped. A limit: grouping by
ways shown by strong signs alone would part them, and is not done.

* The picture checks

** *requantised-look*

Measured on 258 JPEGs (REQUANTISED-MEASURE). Honest, saved once: 82 real
scans and phone pictures taken from the honest sets' PDFs as they keep them,
20 of them measured, the rest clipped or with too few blocks to count, the
largest 0.47 (a phone app's page; a printed half-tone catalogue gave 0.43);
and 56 made from pictures with no JPEG grid of their own, saved once at
quality 60, 75, 85 and 95, 24 measured, the largest 0.30 among those with no
grid. Two of the made-up sources, PNGs a PDF kept, turned out to carry an 8
by 8 grid from an earlier JPEG (their columns' steps twice as large at one
column of eight): saved once more they gave 1.15 to 2.66, a true second
save. Saved twice by ImageMagick (60 then 85, 75 then 90, 85 then 95, 70
then 80, 95 then 85, 90 then 75): 84, 41 measured, 29 at 1.1 or more (up to
4.3), the 12 under 0.8 mostly saved finer first (95 then 85, 90 then 75),
which leaves no gaps. Forged from an honest quality-50 scan and saved at 92:
2.30 to 2.42; the same scan resaved at 95 without change, 2.85, a second
save with an honest cause. Blurred, denoised, made bold or noisy before the
second save: 0.07 to 0.15, the first save's steps gone with the filter. 1.0
sits twice above the honest largest.

An independent review found a phone picture saved again by libjpeg at
quality 90 gives 0.45 to 0.46, near the honest 0.47 above: that honest
picture may itself have been saved twice, finer first. Limits: a picture
whose white is clipped is not measured, a second save among them too, and
the report says it was not measured, never that it was saved once.

** *rendered-paper-most* and *rendered-half-tones-least*

A born-digital page drawn at 150 dpi and saved as one picture: its blank
paper strays by 0.0, and 0.22 of the pixels round its characters are
half-tones. Honest scans read: a flatbed letter 1.42, a quality-50 scan
denoised 8.6, the same scans with contrast raised until paper and ink clip
0.085 and 2.71, a phone page so 0.35. Of the 43 honest PDFs examined (real
scans, phone captures, Swedish office scans) one page was named: an office
copier's scan whose paper it had whitened to one grey (0.00), which the
finding names among its honest causes. The characters' cores were tried
too and left out: a born-digital page's small text has cores of 2 grey
levels, the clipped letter's 0. A scanner set to black and white leaves no
half-tones. A limit: a born-digital page saved as a JPEG (quality 75 or 90)
is not named, its paper strayed by the JPEG's rounding.

** *picture-dates-apart* and *picture-zones-apart*

A camera or scanner writes when a picture was made and when it was changed
together; a minute is left for one that writes them apart. A PNG's time is
universal and an EXIF date names no zone, so the two are set beside each
other within the 26 hours the world's zones span.

** Content credentials and *generator-sizes*

Content credentials are not measured against a value: what they say is
read as written. They were read on 20 picture files of the C2PA reference
library's tests (c2pa-rs): every store read whole, none past a limit of
depth, count or size. One, made by Adobe Firefly, says
trainedAlgorithmicMedia and is worth a look; two test pictures say
algorithmicMedia; ten hold credentials that name no generator, recorded.
Seventeen pictures from an image service that writes credentials
(trainedAlgorithmicMedia, by Flux.1) were all worth a look. Of 233 files
made without a generator (the real scans, the Swedish office scans, the
phone captures, the practice samples, phone and camera pictures, a
photograph editor's exports and screenshots) none was worth a look; one picture kept credentials naming
no generator, recorded. Each was read in under a hundredth of a second.

The sizes image generators often make are named only beside such a finding:
among the honest pictures several are 1024 by 1024 or 2048 by 2048 too.

** *generator-text-cut*

Stable Diffusion's settings follow its prompt in a PNG's text called
parameters, and the note keeps 500 characters of a PNG's text, so a prompt
longer than about 450 hides them. A text of that name 400 characters long
or more counts without its marks. None of those 233 files keeps a PNG
text called parameters, prompt or workflow, or names an image generator in
its programs; made-up pictures of each generator's marks (Stable Diffusion,
a long prompt, ComfyUI, InvokeAI, NovelAI, Midjourney's digital source type,
DALL-E as XMP's CreatorTool) were all worth a look, and honest ones (GIMP's
comment, a short text called parameters, a camera's digitalCapture) none.

Content credentials kept in a PDF were read on the reference library's PDF
written by Adobe Express, whose credentials are those of a picture Adobe
Firefly made (worth a look), and on its two PDFs without credentials
(nothing); the 107 PDFs among the 233 gave nothing.
