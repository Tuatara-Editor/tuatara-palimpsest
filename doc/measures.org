#+title: How the scan measures were chosen

The values of the scan checks were set by reading honest and
made-up scans. What was measured is kept here, by the definition it belongs to,
so that a value can be checked again or changed with its grounds in view.

The honest sets of the last round (November 2026) were 68 real documents: 19
public scans, 46 Swedish office scans from five makers' machines at 150 to 600
dots an inch, and 9 documents photographed with a phone or scanned at home;
292 scanned pictures, 248 of them with words to measure, 30,288 words. Beside
them were 62 PDFs made by programs (51 found on the system: manuals, test pages,
reference cards, written by Distiller, pdfTeX, LuaTeX, groff, Ghostscript, CUPS
and others; 11 written by LibreOffice and other programs), and honest made-up
scans. The forged sets were 46 made-up files (the practice samples, the
contract and the statement, the made-up scans with copies, pages with a word in
another face) and pages made by the tests' own generator for each rule.

* The scan checks

** Black and white pages: BILEVEL-SAMPLES, OTSU-LEVEL, BLACK-AND-WHITE-P

Measured in November 2026 on the honest pages kept as fax (CCITT, group 4
throughout) among the real scans and the forgeries' honest files, 13
documents and 46 pages, on an office scan kept as JBIG2 (9 pages), and on
the forgeries kept as fax (a figure copied within the page, a figure typed
again in a matching face and grain, a stamp from another document, each in
black and white).

Their pictures are drawn by Poppler's pdftoppm from a PDF of the picture
alone. Drawn as a picture of greys, it smooths it: of a test page 600 by 500
pixels, 14,811 pixels came out other than the picture holds, all 256 greys
among them. Drawn as a stencil, every pixel is the picture's own.

Only a picture kept one bit a pixel is judged so: a page stored at eight
bits that holds two to four greys may have been drawn flat by a program,
and is judged as any other. On the honest pages kept as fax, words came out
a pixel or two off their lines (13 words in 7 of the 13 documents, at 200
dots an inch, where a grey scan's are told at 1.5 pixels): where a word sits
is not judged on a black and white page, only how it is turned.

Such a page held no words before: Otsu's level parts black from white at
0, and no pixel is darker than 0. The level is now the middle of the levels
on which no pixel lies, 128. Pages kept as a stencil, as copiers keep the
text layer of a page split into layers, were read with their ink white and
their paper black (96 % of a page's pixels black); they are read with their
ink dark now.

On a black and white page honest prints of a character come out the same
pixel for pixel: up to 89 pairs and groups of them a page on the pages kept
as fax, of up to 250 pixels of ink, and 180 to 338 on the JBIG2 pages, whose
coder prints every character of one shape from one print. The copied figure
of the forgery shares 106 pixels of ink with its source, its nearest other
print 6 pixels off; honest pairs lie 0 to 18 pixels from their nearest other
print. So no size or likeness parts a character copied from an honest one.
A word's ink, edges and paper are one black, a step and one white, and the
roughness of its edges (the pixels where ink meets paper, set beside those
after a majority of 3 by 3) varied from word to word by more on honest pages
(0 to 1.04, a column of figures and a line of small type standing out) than
the retyped figure differed from the honest one in its place (0.62 against
0.65): not used.
Characters the same pixel for pixel lying one offset from as many others,
beside each other in no line: two at most on the honest pages kept as fax,
up to 6 on the JBIG2 pages, which are not searched. A run of three or more,
from one line, is worth a look (*black-and-white-run-least*).

** *scan-measures*

Measured on the honest sets (649 pictures, 231 of real documents scanned, 381 of
real documents photographed with a phone, 37 made up), words named at these
values, and with the least or the multiple three quarters of it, came to the
following. Where it sits: 356, 409, 426. Turned: 0, 0, 0 (7 with the least
halved). Ink: 95, 135, 97. Edges: 332, 448, 498. Paper: 220, 306, 277. Honest
pages have such words (a heading, a line printed in another pass, a form filled
in), which is why each is worth a look at most; the values sit where a quarter
less names up to half as many honest words again. Made up and changed: a word
moved 6 pixels on a bowed page and a column 3 pixels lower are named still with
the least or the multiple half again as large; words pasted into the made-up
scans are named so by their ink, half of them by where they sit, none by their
edges or paper.

Measured again in November 2026 on the 248 honest pictures (30,288 words):
words named where they sit 352, by ink 77, edges 136, paper 192, turned none.
With the least three quarters as large, 418, 101, 185 and 243 (turned 3); with
the multiple three quarters as large, 412, 78, 189 and 206. With either half
again as large: 293 and 248, 23 and 23, 74 and 70, 105 and 92. The forged files'
words named by their edges (0.25 and 6) are all lost with either half again as
large, and those named by their ink half of them; a word pasted into a made-up
scan is named by its ink still with either half again as large, a word lowered
2 to 4 pixels by where it sits. So the edges and the paper stand where a quarter
less names a third more honest words and half again loses the forgeries.

On the honest run of 2026-11-22 (977 files) the 70 real scans' words named
were 369 by where they sit, 31 by ink, 90 by edges and 137 by paper, 28 by a
character off its line, and 3 turned, on 2 real scans (17 turned on the whole
run). How the honest spread of each way sits against the forged values, and
why a word off its line by the baseline alone is now for the record, is under
Honest files worth a look below.

A word lowered by 6 pixels, a third of its height, is not named, and from 7
pixels the line it sits in is named by its edges or paper instead of the word
by where it sits: the word is read as part of a line of its own height, not set
off the line it left. A limit of how lines are read, not of these values.

** Small figures beside a taller label

A word of a line is read when its ink fills two fifths of the line's rows,
so that a dash or a rule standing alone is no word. A total's row can set a
label in tall letters beside an amount in smaller bold figures, higher up,
at the far end: the line then runs from the amount's top to the label's foot,
and the figures fill too few of its rows. Two fifths are now taken of the
page's typical line where a line is taller than it.

On the made-up forgery of a hotel bill (hotel-folio-1) the amount "5,247.89"
had been lost this way since the bands of run-together lines were read in
pieces. Read again, its forged "5" pairs with its source at 1.7 grey levels
and the page is found at the place.

** *band-least*

Measured on the honest sets: 699 pictures, 231 of real documents scanned, 381
photographed with a phone, the rest made up at grain 1 to 3, bold, clipped,
blurred, kept as JPEG, lit unevenly. There was no band on a real document. Three
made-up forms had one, their shaded header row, about 20 levels darker than the
paper above and below it, as honest pages have. That is why a band is worth a
look at most. On the honest run of 2026-11-22 blocks and bands came to 119
findings on 64 files, 69 of them set apart by their paper alone on 42 made-up
honest files, none on a real document and none at the place of a forgery; they
are now for the record (Honest files worth a look, below). Measured on bands of
lines printed again over 13 kinds of made-up page: serif, sans, bold and DejaVu
type, one to four lines, 14 to 28 lines a page, light falling across, JPEG of
quality 75 and 90, 200 dots an inch, kept without loss or as JPEG. Every band
from half its grey to 0.96 of it was named once. A word pasted into it was named
on its own besides. The floor depends on how much of the page the band is, as
the rows about it are its measure. On pages of 21 lines, bands of two lines were
named to 0.97 (seeds 41 to 46). On pages of 12 lines, bands of two or three
lines were named to 0.96, bands of four lines to 0.92, and missed from 0.94
(three seeds). At 0.97 a band is missed on some pages at 200 dots an inch kept
as JPEG of quality 90.

** scan-picture-read

The page's tilt read so was the first reading's on all 179 made-up pages with a
band tried. A band read as it is spoils the reading of the whole page. At 200
dots an inch and half its grey, its paper (123) lies below the midpoint of the
page's darkest ink and its level, so its rows are not left out of the level
(PAGE-LEVEL). It pulls the level from 195, read with it lifted, to 181, and the
page's darkest fiftieth from 114 to 71, against which every word's baseline is
measured. Words far from the band were set off their lines by 1.6 to 1.8 pixels.

** rule-piece-p

Measured on the faces of the made-up scans (Liberation Serif and Sans, regular
and bold, DejaVu Sans): the tallest characters, j, brackets and the bar |, reach
1.35 to 1.4 times the height of the capitals, under half again. The narrowest
stand 2.5 to 4.6 times as tall as wide before the scanner's blur widens them, l
at 2.7 to 3.3, the bar alone past 4. An l stands no taller than the letters that
rise above the others, which UPPER-HEIGHT measures however few they are. So the
bar, and j and brackets in a narrow sans face (3.3 to 3.7 times as tall as
wide), taller by a tenth and narrow, are taken as pieces of rules too, and are
not set beside others for copies: a limit. A piece of a rule 4.2 times as tall
as wide and 1.42 times the type, as a photographed form gave, is told; one cut
to the height of the type, or 2.65 times as tall as wide, is not, and is set
beside the characters as one.

** bare-glyphs

Of 720 honest pages of made-up scans none had fewer than 216 characters
measured. 676 had 2 % or fewer of them without grain, and 28 from 2 to 5 %. 16
had more than a twentieth (small type against a table's rules, bold type the
scanner clipped, prints with no blur), on which words without grain cannot be
told from the page's own. Pages with words typed onto them hold 0.5 to 1.5 %
without grain.

On the real honest pages (170 pages of characters measured) the fewest
characters measured on a page was 152. 125 pages had 2 % or fewer without
grain, 18 from 2 to 5 %, and 27 more than a twentieth, on which nothing is told;
none of them named a word or line without grain. The forged files' pages held
3.1 % at most.

** join-exact-copies: no size makes a pair strong

Pairs the same pixel for pixel on a JPEG's grid, on a stepped page or on a page
of faint grain stay worth a look however much ink they share. Measured in
November 2026, the size of a pair being the pixels of ink of the widest block
the two share:

- The real honest sets (76 files) gave no pair the same pixel for pixel.
- 228 honest pages, made up or taken from real scans. The made-up ones hold
  text in three faces, one face in nine sizes from 7 to 20 pixels regular and
  bold, small type and tables of figures, at grain 1 to 7; 8 pages of real scans
  were taken too. Each was made faint or stepped by a JPEG of quality 30 to 90
  decoded and kept without loss, posterising to 4 to 16 levels, 1-bit made grey
  and saved as a JPEG of quality 50 to 95, JPEG 2000, or kept as a JPEG of
  quality 50 to 95. They gave 925 pairs on 19 pages, of 20 to 48 pixels of ink,
  21 in the middle and 26 at nine tenths, in blocks up to 8 by 10 pixels; on the
  grid of pages kept as a JPEG, 22. Most came from posterising to 12 or 16 levels.
- 84 forged pages of the same kinds, 40 characters each copied with a margin of
  2 to 6 pixels, laid with Darken or pasted, half of them a whole number of
  8-pixel blocks from their source, then saved as a JPEG of quality 50 to 95,
  kept as it is or decoded and kept without loss: 219 pairs, 20 to 97 pixels of
  ink, 26 in the middle, 36 at nine tenths; 9 over 48, 3 of 64 or more.

The honest pairs reach 48, past nine tenths of the copies, so no size keeps them
out and the copies in. The figure of the forged statement found on the grid
shares 64; a limit there would make 3 of 219 copies strong, a third above the
largest honest pair on type 7 to 20 pixels high, where type twice that height
gives honest blocks larger still.

One honest page gave a strong sign: a table of figures at grain 1, saved as a
JPEG of quality 30 and kept without loss, not told stepped, had 19 characters
named copies, figures a whole number of 8-pixel blocks apart that came out the
same (pairs of 22 to 41 pixels of ink). Marking pairs on the grid by their places

place on every page, of letters on a fixed pitch of 8 pixels, of a date stamp's
figures and of copies laid 8 pixels apart; it is not made. The page is told

** GRID-MOVES and *grid-moves-least*: drawn pages saved as JPEGs

A page drawn by a program and saved straight as a JPEG has no grain of its
own: the JPEG's rounding is what makes two prints of a letter differ, and it
rounds two prints drawn at the same place across a pixel alike wherever they
also fall alike on its grid. Words at the same place across on lines a whole
number of blocks apart (left-aligned labels, a table's column) come out the
same pixel for pixel. Where the blur or the type joins two letters into one
mark, the block found the same is two characters wide or more, and the rule
that only narrower pairs on the grid are clean prints named them strong.

Measured in November 2026 on the held-out honest set (24 born-digital PDFs
drawn by poppler at 150, 200 and 300 dots an inch and saved by Pillow at
quality 40, 50 and 65 and with 21 real scanners' tables; 120 pages saved
straight from the drawing, 120 after blur 0.8 and grain 1.5 to 3): 34 of the
pages saved straight gave 37 strong findings, the same on the code before
the JPEG round. Every pair named lay a whole number of blocks apart on the
grid, 16 to 37 pixels wide. Counting, on each picture, the moves between the
pairs found the same on its grid (each move across and down, the same either
way round):

- pages saved straight from the drawing: 2 to 390 moves; the 34 named strong
  37 to 390;
- pages blurred and grained before the save: 0 or 1;
- real scans (8 honest JPEGs of the set, 5 real controls, 2 phone scans, 30
  of the honest run's scans kept as JPEGs): 0;
- the four forged files whose copies are named strong: 0 (their copies lie
  off the grid).

A pair on the grid two characters wide or more is now worth a look when its
picture's pairs on the grid lie at 8 moves or more besides its own. A word
pasted whole blocks from its source repeats at its one move, so on a scan it
stays strong; on a page drawn by a program it is named worth a look, as it
cannot be told from the page's own repeats there.

** picture-likeness

On 939 pages of made-up scans, reading the marks standing alone moved a page's
likeness by 6 % at most, by under 1 % on 19 pages of 20. There, 80 characters
taken at even steps moved it by up to a quarter. Measuring 400 rather than 80
takes a tenth longer on a scan of 60 pages.

** Pages too uneven to judge: *uneven-within* and UNEVEN-COPY-P

On a phone's photograph light, focus and sharpening change from print to
print, and few prints come within 30 grey levels of another of their shape:
4 of 400 on the R17 page, 4 of 270 on R19. Such a page is :uneven and was
not searched for copies at all. Measured within 60 grey levels instead, the
honest pages refused so (34 pictures of the real scans, the Swedish office
scans, the phone scans and the real-based controls) gave likenesses of 29 to
66, 40 to 58 on the phone scans. The same likeness set by bands of four lines
gave the same middle (52 against 51 on R19): the prints differ as much side by
side as across the page, so a region's likeness tells no more than the page's.

The gates of a judged page are far too loose there: alike to half the
likeness, or each other's nearest and twice as near as the next, named
hundreds of honest pairs on those pages, at 8 to 40 grey levels. Each the
other's nearest, alike to a quarter of the likeness, three times as near as
the next of either, and at least half as tall as the type left none on them;
without the height, one pair of specks 3 pixels wide at a phone page's edge
(9 apart, the next at 30). The copy on R17-1 stood at 6.9 against a likeness
of 48, the next of either at 49. A character copied on R19-1 is not found
this way: its source is two letters the blur joined, and the copy, laid
beside another letter, was cut at a neck its source lacks, so the two are
not set beside each other at all.

A review on 227 honest uneven pages made apart (text rendered in ten faces,
printed, photographed with perspective, shadows, sharpening, clipping and
JPEG; mixed pages whose lower half came from a calmer real scan; real scans
photographed again) found eight pairs named by that gate. Six lay in the
calmer half of mixed pages, 8.3 to 12.8 apart against likenesses of 47 to
53: the page's middle is no measure of a calmer part. One was two bold t's
whose ink was clipped to one grey, compared over 45 pixels of edge; one two
strokes 4 by 14 pixels down a monospace column. So a pair is now judged
against the lesser of the page's likeness and that of the prints whose rows
meet either's (LIKENESS-ABOUT, 5 of them near one of their shape at
least: the two pairs left at 10 had 7 and 8, their lines alike to 22 and
31); it is compared over 60 pixels at least once clipped ink is set
aside (at first a fixed floor: the copy on R17-1 over 217, the bold t's 45,
the monospace strokes 26 to 44); and each is at least half as wide as a
typical character. The same review found side effects of giving such a
page's characters a likeness: words without grain were sought there (three
honest findings), and its prints became peers of judged pages up to two
pages away, which moved a judged page's pair on one Swedish scan. Such a
page's characters are now set beside its own only (APART-PEERS) and kept
out of BARE-WORDS and BARE-MARKS.

A re-check found what that cost and what it left open. A letter brought from
a judged page onto an uneven one was no longer sought: an uneven page's
characters are now set beside the judged pages' prints too, one way, and a
pair across them named when alike to a quarter of the judged page's
likeness. A word copied within a short line put its other letters among the
prints about the pair and pulled that likeness down to the copies': the
characters whose nearest lies from them as the pair lie from each other are
now left out (MOVED-ALIKE-P), and a line left with fewer than 5 for that
reason is judged by the page; one as short of its own accord (the invoice
row of a mixed page, 3 letters beside the pair at 11.6) names nothing. Each
print about the pair is set first beside the prints of its own line: a line
from a calmer source, set beside the page, found its nearest among the
page's uneven prints at about 48 and let an honest pair at 3.3 through. On a
page whose lines hold few prints near one another (a word copied and saved
at quality 88: 3 of 56 within 60 on their own lines) the page's prints are
taken instead, but for strokes alone (SHAPELESS-GLYPH-P): two stems 4 by 17
in the calmer half of a mixed page, 12.1 apart, had a line of 14 prints none
near another and the page's 51 behind them, so a stroke is judged by its own
line or not at all. The width rule lost copied i, l, t and figures 1, and the fixed 60
pixels and a type height over the whole file failed a phone page at a smaller
scale than the scans beside it. Both gave way to rules on each picture's own
type: half its taller type's height, and a quarter of its typical
character's ink compared (*uneven-counted-share*; a made-up figure 1 came
to 0.43). The honest bold t's and monospace strokes were both marks without
grain of their own (GLYPH-BARE), clipped flat, and pairs of two such marks
are left out instead.

** Pages drawn on a grid: RULED-GRID-P and *ruled-grain-most*

Born-digital pages drawn over a grid every 8 or 16 pixels, without grain,
had their copies strong (26 groups on main, 8 after once-JPEG pages were
told): the grid crosses each print where it falls, so their prints differ
(alike to 10 to 17 grey levels, where the same page without the grid gives
0) and two are the same where they fall alike on it. Their blank paper reads
as straying by 8 to 13 for the grid lines, so they were not drawn by a
program. The paper is now also measured pixel by pixel, each against the
mean of its two neighbours across, down or on either slant, the least of the
four: a line drawn runs the same along its length. Pages drawn on grids give
0 to 0.38, with grain of 0.5 added 0.15 to 0.85; scans of grain 2 and more
give more, and many real scans whose paper was whitened 0. A grid in the
blank paper (RULED-GRID-P) is told by the mean greys of its columns and its
rows, gathered by place within every 8 or 16 pixels, lying 4 grey levels apart
or more: every page drawn on a grid of 8 or 16 and none of the 264 real
scanned pictures. A page with both is drawn by a program, its characters not
judged. One page saved as a JPEG of quality 30 strays by 3.5 for the JPEG's
ringing and stays strong in one pair, as on main: a limit.

** *likeness-same-most* and FLAT-COPIES: prints that share their ink

Pages made black and white without dithering from scans of grain 3, then
smoothed by 0.7 pixels, gave 12 and 43 characters strong: two prints are the
same wherever their black and white fell alike, and differ elsewhere by 14 to
22 grey levels, judged as prints. The nearest prints of a character on such
pages share 0.82 to 0.94 of their ink grey level for grey level; on 185 real
scanned pictures 0.02 to 0.09, on made-up scans of grain 1 to 7 up to 0.31,
on posterised pages 0.43 to 0.98. A group of copies is worth a look
(FLAT-COPIES) when, for half its characters or more, the prints on the
character's own line, in no group of copies nor stamp, share half their ink
or more with their nearest; where a line holds fewer than three such, the
prints in groups but for those the same in all their ink, else the five
nearest its row in no group. Judged over eight type heights, a group on the
last line of a black and white band took in the grey lines below it and
stayed strong (19 characters on one honest page). Judged by line, the
honest pages made black and white in their top fifth to two thirds give no
group strong, and copies with grain laid 1 to 10 type heights below such a
band, or in its grey part, stay strong (22 to 24 characters): a line of
copies laid whole holds no prints but copies, and the copies the same in all
their ink are left out. A stamp's figures drawn alike are left out too.

** jpeg-history: a picture kept without loss that was once a JPEG

A JPEG leaves every frequency of its 8 by 8 blocks near a whole number, on the
grid it had, however fine its quality; JPEG-HISTORY measures the mean cosine
of a whole turn times each frequency 2 or more from nothing, at the best of
the 64 places the grid can begin, and how many standard deviations that sum
stands above frequencies spread evenly. Measured in November 2026, a picture
counted as told when 5 standard deviations or more:

- Never saved as JPEGs were 96 made-up scans (text, tables of figures, one face in
  nine sizes, bold and small type at grain 0 to 3, grain 0 born digital with
  and without blur, posterised to 4 to 16 levels, 1-bit with and without

** Copies saved again at the page's own steps: RESAVED-COPIES

Measured in November 2026. A JPEG decoded and saved again with the same
steps comes back as it was, so a character copied from elsewhere on the
page, then saved so, is what that save makes of its source laid there: the
source is all but unchanged, and the copy, rounded on a grid its source
never sat on, is no longer alike pixel for pixel. The nearest print of the
character's shape, moved by up to a pixel either way, is laid over its box
and two pixels round it in the page's decoded blocks; the blocks are saved
with the file's own table on its grid and decoded again (RESAVED-COPY), and
the character's box is set beside the result.

The test is in truth whether the source falls within the rounding of the
copy's blocks. Where the steps are coarse beside the prints' grain, or the
prints were drawn alike, any print laid over another may fall within it
and come back as it. So a character is named only when, besides coming
within half of the way and a quarter of the page's likeness:

- none of the other prints of its shape nearest it comes as near
  (*resaved-controls*, 4, within 30 grey levels): only its source gives it
  back;
- of up to 60 characters spread over its page, each set beside its nearest
  print the same way, 20 or more are measured and no more than a fiftieth
  come as near (*resaved-page-sample*, *resaved-page-least*,
  *resaved-page-most*): the page's steps leave honest prints apart;
- both have 100 pixels of ink or more, are not a whole number of blocks
  apart, and the page's prints differ enough to be judged at all.

- The forged-scan set's six copies kept in a JPEG-in-PDF by the scanner's
  writer (Pillow, quality 70 to 85): 0.55 to 1.77 grey levels from the

- A copy saved by libjpeg (Pillow) on a made-up page of grain 5 at quality

** DRAWN-STAMP-P and *stamp-grain-most*

A stamp drawn by software whose figures include a plain one, a bar as a 1 is,
was strong: plain strokes are never judged copies, so the 1s were in no group
and the stamp failed the rule that every repeated shape lies in one group.
Prints of a shape the same pixel for pixel now count as drawn too; a rubber
stamp pressed and scanned never prints a figure twice the same pixel for pixel,
and one with a figure copied over another stays strong.

Shapes are told by the ink of the prints, not by their size alone: figures of
one size but of different shapes lie 76 to 187 grey levels apart (the root of
the mean squared difference of their ink), prints of one drawn figure 0, and
prints of one figure of a rubber stamp pressed and scanned 16 to 18, under the
20 a shape takes in. So a figure printed once, a 6 among 0s, stands alone and
does not count against the stamp. A stamp needs one shape repeated, not two:
a date stamp of 2026 repeats only its 2.

Grain is judged for each group of copies apart (DRAWN-GRAIN-P), against the
page's characters of the group's size outside any stamp: judged over the

** REGREYED-DISTANCE and *regreyed-share*

The paper within a stamp's figures alone let a forger hide copies: digits of
the page cut out onto white (every pixel lighter than 150 made white), made
red and laid twice each into a red stamp drawn in the page's face, at grain 6
and 7, were named a stamp drawn by software. The source match now judges a
copy's ink alone, its pixels darker than three quarters of the way to its
lightest grey, against each character of its page not in a strong group of
copies, laid middle to middle and up to a pixel and a half either way by
half pixels, and also turned by the turn measured of its words. Prints of
one figure, the same pixel for pixel (SAME-PIXELS-P), are left out: they are
found as copies of each other. A copy whose ink differs from its source by a
constant alone fits it at about 0 and is kept. A group matching its source
within *regreyed-share* of the page's paper grain is never taken as drawn.

The distance is set beside how far the paper within the page's characters
strays (GLYPH-PAPER-GRAIN), as both grow with the scanner's grain: a fixed
bound of 3 grey levels lost the cut-outs from grain 8 on, and 6 took a
blurred stamp for copies. As a share of that paper's grain, cut-out copies
made red from one of their colours lie 0.86 to 0.92 from their source at
grain 6 to 9 (the reviewers' pages; 0.05 to 0.09 on the tests' pages, whose
colours carry one grain). Honest stamps drawn in the page's face lie 2.9 or
more when sharp (140 groups: the page's face and bold faces, red, blue and
black, 0 to 15 degrees, grain 1.5 to 7) and, blurred as the scan is (0.5 to
1.2 pixels) or drawn finer and scaled down, 1.12 and 1.15 on the reviewers'
pages at grain 6 and 7 and 1.45 or more on the tests' at grain 5 and 7.
*regreyed-share* is 1, by 0.08 above the cut-outs and 0.12 under the blurred
stamps. At grain 1 to 3, where most real scans lie, neither reached this
rule: the blurred stamps and the copies are worth a look there by the faint
grain alone. With it, the cut-outs at grain 6 to 9, in a stamp or laid as an
amount alone, are strong; on main the amount alone was worth a look. Copies
turned 7 to 10 degrees lie 10 to 13 grey levels from theirs at grain 7 and

What still hid them was a recolouring that scales the grain further.
Green and blue at 0.6 to 0.7 of the grey: laid upright the copies stay
strong by their source (REGREYED-DISTANCE), but turned 8 to 10 degrees they
were worth a look, as light copies (LIGHT-COPIES) or a stamp drawn by
software (DRAWN-GRAIN-P), their grain read as a program's. Each such rule
is overruled by the copy's source, and the source match reached no turn: on
the tests' page a light copy turned 1 degree was already worth a look,
bilinear reading of a turned copy leaving its ink 1.7 to 7.5 of the paper's
grain from its source, and the turn measured of a word of four figures was

The grain of the paper a copy carries survives both: a recolouring scales it, a
turn moves it, but pixel for pixel it is still the source's. The copy is laid
where its ink fits a character of its page best, turned up to 15 degrees either
way (by whole degrees and pixels, then quarter degrees and half pixels about the
best), and what each pixel of its box lies from the middle of its 9 is set
beside the same in the source where it falls, as a correlation, each value past
three times the middle of its kind counted as three times.

A first version took every pixel whose 9 were paper, and an independent review
found it read shape: honest stamps in the page's face followed the page's
letters by 0.46 to 0.59, where chance gives 0, the tails of a smoothed edge and
the curves the best fit shares counted as grain, and only the gate of 16 pixels
kept such stamps from 0.75. So now only pixels two or more from the copy's ink
count (the 25 about each all paper), and only where the source's 9 are paper
too. A streak a scanner draws down or across the page lies in every print in
those columns or rows, so pixels of a column or row of the box holding fewer
than 3 are left out and the mean of each column and then of each row is taken
out of both; that leaves free the pixels less the columns and rows and one more,
and the bound is taken for those. A placement turned less than 2 degrees on the
same 8-pixel step as the copy is passed over, as a JPEG's blocks ring alike on
its grid. Of the characters the copy fits best (400 by size, turned or not, ties
taken top to bottom and left to right, then the 40 its ink fits best at a
glance, by whole degrees and then a whole pixel either way at the best of them,
then the 5 best laid in full, *grain-candidates-most* and *grain-sources-most*)
the closest must reach SHARED-GRAIN-BOUND, 0.75 over 20 free pixels or more and
more over fewer (as far as four times the spread a correlation has by chance,
0.80 at 16), and lie *shared-grain-margin* 0.3 above the next: a shape alike in
every print of a letter fits them all alike, a copy's grain only one. Where no
other scores no margin can be shown, and the match is never taken: a false
strong is worse than a miss. A copy is laid on some 40,500 placements at most.

** Characters far lighter than the text: FAINT-GLYPHS and LIGHT-COPIES

Copies of a page's characters made lighter by 60, 90 and 100 grey levels

took 5 to 17.6 seconds and up to 6 GB before. Made lighter by 60 and 90, alone or beside a stamp,

| a 3 by 3 median                            | 1.00 to 1.88              |
| blurred by a Gaussian of 1.5 pixels        | 1.69 to 3.83              |

A page blurred grows its edges as a page made larger does, and loses as
much detail when it is made half as large and back: 0.023 to 0.060 of the
span from paper to ink blurred, 0.025 to 0.052 made a quarter as large and
four times again, 0.056 to 0.221 as it was. The grain of the blank paper
set beside its neighbour's was missing on most pages, their paper made
white, and where it was there it overlapped: 0.07 to 0.51 as they were,
0.42 to 0.71 made twice as large, 0.44 to 0.89 made four times as large.
Honest pages at 400 and 600 dots an inch blurred by a Gaussian of 1.2
pixels, as a scanner whose optics are softer than its pixels blurs, came to
2.1 to 2.3. So a page whose edges are wider than *edge-scanned-widest* (2.0)
is said to hold the detail of a lower resolution: made larger, blurred, or
scanned by such a scanner, for which the span of resolutions given does not
hold.

The resolution it holds the detail of is given as from its pixels' times
0.6 over its edges' width (the sharpest edge a picture can have, a step
within a pixel, which a page made smaller keeps) to its pixels' times 1.9
over it (the widest honest edge). A page whose edges are as narrow as a
scan's is said to have edges as sharp as a scan's at the resolution of its
pixels: scanned at that, made smaller from a finer scan, or made larger from no less than its pixels'
times 0.6 over its edges' width. It is never said flatly to be scanned at
that resolution: pages made smaller keep their edges as sharp, and sharp
pages made 1.33 to 2 times larger with bicubic, Lanczos or Mitchell blending
came to edges of 1.2 to 2.0. Of 257 made pages whose resolution before is
known, what was said held for 256. The one it missed was made
twice as large by repeating its pixels, whose edges stay sharp; such a page
has every change from one pixel to the next on one place in every two, and
is named by that (REPEATED-PIXELS).

The pixels to the inch a JPEG says disagreed with the page's on 4 honest
pages, all saying 96, what programs write when given none. The letters of
the honest pages, their capitals' height at the page's pixels to the inch,
came to 6.2 to 9.6 points on pages of text and up to 48 on pages of
headings, too spread to tell a wrong page size by.

Working it out adds about 0.06 s a million pixels to reading a scanned
page: 0.18 s for an A4 page at 150 dots an inch, 0.53 s at 300, 1.8 s at
600.

** judge-tail

Of the same 15,475 honest PDFs, 14,686 ended their last %%EOF with a line
feed, 447 with a carriage return and line feed, 101 with a carriage return
and 230 with nothing; none of the 20 made for the round, and none of the
others, had a single octet of white space after that line end: no newlines
more, no spaces, no padding with NULs. A mail program or a transfer in text
mode may add one more line end, so four octets or fewer of line ends and
spaces are nothing. More is worth a look, as white space that can hold data:
a few hundred NULs or spaces carry a file's worth of bits. A kilobyte or more
is strong, as Adobe's readers look for %%EOF only in a file's last 1,024
octets, so no program whose files open there writes it; the 1.4 MB a reviewer
hid there is far past it. So are 64 octets or more mixing two or more kinds
of white space (spaces, tabs, NULs, form feeds, carriage returns, line feeds),
the way a code hiding bits in white space writes them (eight octets' worth of
bits at one a character; 900 octets of carriage returns and line feeds at
random hold about 110): no honest file had even one of them there. Of the
113 honest scans and samples kept for testing (real-scans, real-scans-sweden,
palimpsest-scans, palimpsest-samples, my-phone-scans), none has a single octet
after the line end of its last %%EOF; the one sample with a tail is made to
show data hidden there. Mixed white space is named by its kinds, each counted,
and stays strong when a few other octets lie among it: ten Ctrl-Z after 1.4 MB
of mixed white space were worth a look before, as data too short to hold a
sentence. One kind of white space with fewer than 16 other octets stays worth
a look at any length: NULs to a block's end and a Ctrl-Z or two, as carved
slack holds, are not mixed; among other octets, white space counts as mixed
only with eight octets or more of a second kind, so a line end before the
Ctrl-Z does not make such padding strong.

One octet repeated, after one line end at most, holds no data at any length,
so it is worth a look, named as padding: a file copied to a block's size or
carved out of a disk ends with NULs to the end of its cluster (3,431 in a
4,096-octet cluster), and old transfers padded with Ctrl-Z (0x1A) to a
128-octet block. Those were strong at 1 KB, or at 16 octets for Ctrl-Z, which
is not white space; with a single octet repeated there is nothing to read.

** GENERATOR-GRID, *generator-grid-effect*, *generator-grid-share* and *generator-grid-least*

Measured in November 2026 on pictures an image generator made, 68 pages of eight
kinds (letters, invoices, forms, receipts, statements, memos, certificates,
identity cards) in four looks (a flat scan, a phone photograph of paper, a fax,
coloured paper), 6 earlier pages and 8 renders at 512 by 704, all made by
Z-Image-Turbo through stable-diffusion.cpp, whose decoder draws each cell of 8
by 8 pixels; and 50 copies of 20 of them: printed and scanned again (grey,
contrast stretched, blurred by 0.6 to 1.2 pixels, turned 0.6 to 2 degrees, given
noise, saved as a JPEG of quality 75 to 90), saved as JPEGs of quality 60 and
80, and put in PDFs as a scanner writes them (one JPEG of quality 82 to 88). The
honest sets were those of the last round (134 files, the real scans, the Swedish
office scans, the phone scans, the made-up scans and samples, the forgeries'
honest files), with 111 pictures more kept without loss: honest pages made 0.83,
1.37 and 2 times their size and saved as PNGs, and 28 pictures a Fedora system
keeps (wallpapers, screenshots, textures, icons).

A first threshold of 15 times chance on the strength alone named honest pages: a
JPEG scan cropped by a few pixels and saved as a JPEG again, then kept without
loss, has two grids out of step, and they multiply into a pattern of 8 by 8
pixels changing along rows and columns at once, as a decoder's does. The
strength grows with the flat pixels a page holds, so on a large page any lasting
pattern passes it. So the strength is now only a bound against chance (4, which
49 times is a chi-square of 49 degrees of freedom, beaten by chance less than
once in a million million pictures), and the pattern is judged by its size
against the picture's own noise: the root of the mean of its squared offsets
over the root of the mean of the flat pixels' own, the same at every size of
page. A picture once saved as a JPEG (JPEG-HISTORY) is not measured at all.

Measured again with that rule, besides the sets above, on 390 pages made from 30
honest JPEG scans: each decoded as it is and kept as a PNG; its paper made grey
(white taken to 93 % and 97 %) and saved as a JPEG of quality 50, 75 and 92; and
cropped by 3 and 5, 4 and 4, and 1 and 0 pixels and saved as a JPEG of quality
75 and 90, each kept as a PNG; with an independent review's crops and dithers,
and ordered dithers (8 by 8 and 4 by 4, 64 levels) and a half-tone screen on
grey paper.

The generator's pictures kept without loss gave 0.016 to 0.109; with the least
at 0.075, 46 of the 76 measured are named (62 with the first rule), those named
92 % to 124 % along both at once. Of the 390 pages, 132 were once JPEGs by their
history and not measured, 134 had no flat pixels (their paper white), 138 were
measured and none named: the most 0.054 (with 24 % along both at once), and
0.049 where 84 % ran along both at once (a crop by 4 and 4 at quality 75 whose
history was not told). With the first rule 5 of them were named. Of the other
293 honest pictures measured none is named, none above 0.05. Pages made up with
even stripes of text, cropped and saved again, gave 0.08 to 0.098 but at most 60
% along both at once. Ordered dither on grey paper gave 3.1 to 17 and the
half-tone screen 0.255, so the most, 0.15, keeps them out; on white paper they
leave no flat pixels and are not measured. In all, of 431 honest pictures
measured, none is named.

A second review made blank grey paper with noise of 0.8 to 1.6 grey levels,
saved it as a JPEG of quality 60 to 75, cropped it by a few pixels and saved it
at 90 to 95, kept as a PNG: with no text, JPEG-HISTORY tells nothing, the first
JPEG smooths the paper's noise down to 0.15 to 1.06 (its offsets' root mean
square), and the pattern of the two grids, set over so little noise, came to
0.077 to 0.25: 22 of 74 were named. So the pattern is judged only where the flat
pixels' noise is 1.5 or more (*generator-grid-noise-least*). The generator's
pictures measured gave 1.29 to 6.4, those named 2.17 and up; the 431 honest
pictures measured 0.05 to 9 (a tenth under 0.56, the middle 3.9, 99 under 1.5);
real scans 0.69 to 3.3 in the review's sets. And a place of the grid must hold
2,000 flat pixels (*generator-grid-flat-least*, 300 before): below a few
thousand, a pattern just above chance comes near 0.1 against the noise by chance
alone. That leaves 69 of the 82 generator's pictures measured, 45 named (one
fax-look page lost), and no honest picture named.

Pictures kept as JPEGs, or as JPEGs in a TIFF (its Compression 6 or 7), are not
measured. Their own blocks leave a pattern of 8 by 8 pixels, and the JPEG round
takes the generator's away: the 20 JPEG copies, the 10 scanner-style PDFs and
the 20 prints scanned again measured 0.8 to 16 times chance, at most 28 % along
both at once, so none would be named. A print and scan, or any JPEG save,
removes this sign.

It costs 16 ms on a picture of 768 by 1024 and 28 to 30 ms on a page of 2,473 by
3,497 pixels, and makes no garbage for each pixel.

** Why it is never strong

The finding was first strong when all of these held: the file names the
producer and creator of two genuine files or more, to the letter; those agree
in a strict habit; it differs there; and the producer names a version. Then
neither a software update nor another office explains it: both change the name
or the version. But two blind controls, 062 (mutool clean) and 084 (qpdf),
written again with nothing changed, met it as the forgeries did. A file only
written again by a tool that tidies files or by a portal is not the issuer's
as it stands, and the finding cannot tell it from a changed one, so it is
worth a look. Its words still say more when the conditions above hold.

The harsher test, one program on many desks instead of one issuer
(=habits-strong.py=): every PDF of the honest run, 623, of which 190 stand in
groups of three or more by producer and creator; each set beside all others of
its group. Two would stand apart in a strict habit. One is a scan whose
scanner names itself without a version: an update cannot show in such a name,
and the version rule was added for it (worth a look, not strong). The other is
an authority's scan from an office copier, prepared for the web with a
cross-reference stream where six other authorities' scans from that model have
a table: written again by a web publishing system that kept the copier's name.
That is what the finding says, and its honest cause, an archive or a portal
that writes every file again, is named in it. One of 190, across issuers.

A producer differing only in its version is for the record (an update),
unless a strict habit differs as well: then worth a look, since an update
seldom changes how a program puts files together and a version-only finding
would otherwise hide a rewrite.
