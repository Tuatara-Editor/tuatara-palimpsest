#+title: How the scan measures were chosen

The values of the scan checks were set by reading honest and
made-up scans. What was measured is kept here, by the definition it belongs to,
so that a value can be checked again or changed with its grounds in view.

* The scan checks

** page-level

On the honest sets (699 pictures) such rows were found on 113, nearly all
photographed with a phone. The level moved on 47 of them, by 1 to 3 grey levels
but on 7, by up to 24. The words read on the 113, their paper and ink read
without those rows too (PICTURE-WORDS), were 3,845 before and 3,820 after, and
1,022 and 1,025 on those scanned.

** *scan-measures*

Measured on the honest sets (649 pictures, 231 of real documents scanned, 381 of
real documents photographed with a phone, 37 made up), words named at these
values, and with the least or the multiple three quarters of it, came to the
following. Where it sits: 356, 409, 426. Turned: 0, 0, 0 (7 with the least
halved). Ink: 95, 135, 97. Edges: 332, 448, 498. Paper: 220, 306, 277. Honest
pages have such words (a heading, a line printed in another pass, a form filled
in), which is why each is worth a look at most; the values sit where a quarter
less names up to half as many honest words again. Made up and changed: a word
moved 6 pixels on a bowed page and a column 3 pixels lower are named still with
the least or the multiple half again as large; words pasted into the made-up
scans are named so by their ink, half of them by where they sit, none by their
edges or paper.

** *band-least*

Measured on the honest sets: 699 pictures, 231 of real documents scanned, 381
photographed with a phone, the rest made up at grain 1 to 3, bold, clipped,
blurred, kept as JPEG, lit unevenly. There was no band on a real document. Three
made-up forms had one, their shaded header row, about 20 levels darker than the
paper above and below it, as honest pages have. That is why a band is worth a
look at most. Measured on bands of lines printed again over 13 kinds of made-up
page: serif, sans, bold and DejaVu type, one to four lines, 14 to 28 lines a
page, light falling across, JPEG of quality 75 and 90, 200 dots an inch, kept
without loss or as JPEG. Every band from half its grey to 0.96 of it was named
once. A word pasted into it was named on its own besides. The floor depends on
how much of the page the band is, as the rows about it are its measure. On pages
of 21 lines, bands of two lines were named to 0.97 (seeds 41 to 46). On pages of
12 lines, bands of two or three lines were named to 0.96, bands of four lines to
0.92, and missed from 0.94 (three seeds). At 0.97 a band is missed on some pages
at 200 dots an inch kept as JPEG of quality 90.

** *scan-region-paper*

On the honest sets (649 pictures) blocks of ink named at 1 grey level, 2 more
than at 2; at 3 or 4, as many as at 2, and every band of the made-up series
named still.

** scan-picture-read

The page's tilt read so was the first reading's on all 179 made-up pages with a
band tried. A band read as it is spoils the reading of the whole page. At 200
dots an inch and half its grey, its paper (123) lies below the midpoint of the
page's darkest ink and its level, so its rows are not left out of the level
(PAGE-LEVEL). It pulls the level from 195, read with it lifted, to 181, and the
page's darkest fiftieth from 114 to 71, against which every word's baseline is
measured. Words far from the band were set off their lines by 1.6 to 1.8 pixels.

** rule-piece-p

Measured on the faces of the made-up scans (Liberation Serif and Sans, regular
and bold, DejaVu Sans): the tallest characters, j, brackets and the bar |, reach
1.35 to 1.4 times the height of the capitals, under half again. The narrowest
stand 2.5 to 4.6 times as tall as wide before the scanner's blur widens them, l
at 2.7 to 3.3, the bar alone past 4. An l stands no taller than the letters that
rise above the others, which UPPER-HEIGHT measures however few they are. So the
bar, and j and brackets in a narrow sans face (3.3 to 3.7 times as tall as
wide), taller by a tenth and narrow, are taken as pieces of rules too, and are
not set beside others for copies: a limit. A piece of a rule 4.2 times as tall
as wide and 1.42 times the type, as a photographed form gave, is told; one cut
to the height of the type, or 2.65 times as tall as wide, is not, and is set
beside the characters as one.

** grain-levels

on the practice scans honest characters stray by 5 or more where the typical is
17 to 19

** bare-glyphs

Of 720 honest pages of made-up scans none had fewer than 216 characters
measured. 676 had 2 % or fewer of them without grain, and 28 from 2 to 5 %. 16
had more than a twentieth (small type against a table's rules, bold type the
scanner clipped, prints with no blur), on which words without grain cannot be
told from the page's own. Pages with words typed onto them hold 0.5 to 1.5 %
without grain.

** same-strips

Of 720 honest pages the most strips of one number on a page with grain was 11,
and 291 to 333 on pages drawn by a program set in a file beside a scan.

** picture-likeness

On 939 pages of made-up scans, reading the marks standing alone moved a page's
likeness by 6 % at most, by under 1 % on 19 pages of 20. There, 80 characters
taken at even steps moved it by up to a quarter. Measuring 400 rather than 80
takes a tenth longer on a scan of 60 pages.

** stepped-picture-p

On honest made-up pages so made, of grain 2 to 3 grey levels and judged by their
likeness, a JPEG of quality 30 kept without loss made a pair of prints the same
pixel for pixel on 5 of 6, 16 levels on 5 of 6.

** copied-glyphs

On the made-up scans so set the paper strays by 0 to 0.4 and the cores by 13 to
30.

Scanned paper not made white strays by nearly 1 or more (0.97 on a denoised
page). The tallest characters of type (a bar, brackets, a j) are 1.32 to 1.36
times the taller type in the faces tried (DejaVu, Nimbus Sans Narrow,
Liberation Serif); the stamps tried stand at 1.97, signatures and bar codes at
2.0 to 2.1, so a group taller by half than the type is taken as laid on.

** check-scans

On honest made-up pages made with a JPEG kept without loss or few grey levels,
of grain 0.7 to 4 grey levels, 32 of 169 had a pair of prints the same pixel for
pixel. Honest pages of small print are read at about three fifths of the
characters their ink is reckoned to hold, so a page is named as partly read
only below half.

** split-joined

In the faces tried (DejaVu Serif, Sans, Condensed and Mono, Nimbus Sans Narrow,
Liberation Serif, bold too) a W, an M, an m, an @ or a % is 1.5 to 2.2 times the
typical width, and two narrow letters run together (il, ii, ij) 0.8 to 1.4
times it.

* The content checks

** check-page-content

The shapes covering text in the honest files read were wholly opaque: a
spreadsheet's white text box laid over a cell, 25 by 216 points, and
LibreOffice's text boxes in Calc and Writer, 119 by 14 and 113 by 17 points, so
no value of opacity parts honest from not. The honest scans read cover 100 to
100.4 % of their page, the largest picture of any other page 70 % of it (a
figure); no honest file of a scan with text recognition was at hand to measure.

** *maker-trailer-marks*

46 honest files LibreOffice 26.2.6.3 made match their /DocChecksum, and every
file written again or changed after does not; no output of an older version was
at hand. Word's hybrid cross-reference (/XRefStm) and Distiller were looked at
and not added: not every version writes them.

** *replacement-size* and *replacement-baseline*

In the 26 made-up files read that set new text over old with LibreOffice or a
script, the new text has the old one's size to the hundredth of a point, and
lies 0 to 0.51 points off the old line. The honest text boxes read set their
text at the size beneath or 19.8 % apart, so size alone does not part them;
office programs set a text box's text 1.55 points off the line beneath (a
Writer callout), 2.85 (Writer text boxes), 3.63 (Impress) and 3.77 (a Calc text
box). One point lies between the two, nearer the made-up files than the closest
honest box.

** check-ends

Of the 105 honest files read (books, manuals and notes, and files written by
LibreOffice, pdfTeX, LuaTeX, Ghostscript, Cairo, MuPDF and ImageMagick) none
had data after the last %%EOF. A made-up file with data hidden after its end
has 49 octets.

** white-p*

The shapes covering text in the made-up files read are pure white (1.0); so is
the one honest file read whose shape covers text (a spreadsheet's white text
box laid over a cell).

** check-fonts

The fonts of the 10 honest files read that date both were dated 165 days or
more before their document; a made-up file with a font put in later dates it
18.7 days after.

** check-metadata

Of the honest files read (books, manuals and notes, and files written by
LibreOffice, pdfTeX, LuaTeX, Ghostscript, Cairo, MuPDF and ImageMagick), the 10
that give both dates in both places give them to the second, the 23 that give
both dates give none changed before made, and the newest date was 5 seconds
before it was read. A made-up file with its metadata edited gives dates 11.7
and 46 days apart, and changed 25 hours before made. A minute leaves room for a
program that writes the two at different moments; a day, for a time zone
written wrong, up to 14 hours.
